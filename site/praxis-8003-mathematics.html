Praxis 8003 Mathematics: What the Test Covers and How to Prepare

2026-09-15 · Last verified 2026-09-15 · Reading time: about 10 min
Test requirements vary by state, preparation program, and effective date. Confirm your required test code with your state licensing agency or educator preparation program before registering.

Praxis 8003 — officially Elementary Education Fundamentals: Mathematics — is the mathematics test in ETS’s redesigned Elementary Education Fundamentals family. It is a 100-minute, 68-question test[1][5]. Unlike the reading tests in this family, 8003 includes numeric-entry questions and provides an on-screen scientific calculator[1].

This page explains what the test covers, who it is for, how the three content domains are weighted, what the calculator and numeric-entry rules actually say, and how to confirm whether it is the test you should register for. It also includes a free 30-question practice mini test with explanations — including four questions you answer by typing a number rather than choosing an option.

Is Praxis 8003 the Right Test for You?

This is the most important question on this page, and it is not one we can answer for you.

Praxis 8003 is a subject test within the Elementary Education Fundamentals family. Candidates who need it are typically in one of these situations:

  • Their state or preparation program requires an elementary mathematics content test and has adopted 8003 for that purpose.
  • They need to demonstrate mathematics content knowledge for an elementary licensure pathway that is moving to the 8000 series.
  • They are adding an elementary mathematics endorsement and must show current content knowledge in the subject.

What we can verify, and what we cannot. We have verified qualifying scores for 8003 in two states. In both, 8003 is a listed requirement with its own cut score:

StateQualifying score for Praxis 8003Source
West Virginia152ETS state requirements page[2]
Arkansas136ETS state requirements page[3]

For the remaining 48 states, adoption status and cut scores are not verified. We do not publish numbers we have not confirmed against a state agency or ETS state requirements page, because a wrong answer here costs you a test fee and a testing window.

How to confirm your test code in about five minutes:

  1. Open your state’s ETS requirements page: https://praxis.ets.org/state-requirements/<state>-tests.html. (State names are written as one word, no hyphen — westvirginia, not west-virginia.)
  2. Search the page for Elementary Education Fundamentals: Mathematics and note the code and the qualifying score. If your state still lists 5003, that is the test you need until it retires[5].
  3. If the page lists both an old and a new code, contact your educator preparation program or state licensing agency and ask which code applies to your certification pathway.

If you are unsure after both steps, do not register yet. The fee is $79.00 and retake windows are limited[1].

The 8000 series is one family of tests. If you are still working out how it replaces the older series, our Praxis Elementary Education Fundamentals hub maps the whole family, and 5001 vs. the 8000 series covers the transition mechanics.

What Praxis 8003 Actually Tests

Most elementary mathematics preparation is organized by grade band — what third graders learn, then what fourth graders learn. ETS organizes Praxis 8003 by content domain instead, and the three domains carry different weights[4][5]. Studying by grade level is therefore a poor fit for this test: the same domain spans several grades, and the largest domain is not the one candidates usually expect.

Content domainQuestionsShare of the test
Numbers and Operations28about 41%
Algebraic Thinking20about 29%
Geometry, Measurement and Data20about 29%

Numbers and Operations — 28 questions (about 41%)

The largest domain, and the one that decides most outcomes. Expect place value and the base-ten system, the four operations with whole numbers, fractions and decimals as numbers rather than procedures, ratio and percent reasoning, and the properties of operations used to justify a method.

Typical question shapes:

  • Identify a student’s misconception from a sample of their work, then choose the representation that addresses the reasoning behind it.
  • Decide which model (area model, number line, fraction strip) makes a given procedure visible.
  • Interpret a quotient with a remainder in the context of a word problem.

Algebraic Thinking — 20 questions (about 29%)

Patterns and rules, equivalent expressions, the meaning of the equals sign, one-variable equations, and proportional reasoning. The emphasis is on relationships and representation rather than on symbolic manipulation for its own sake.

Typical question shapes:

  • Find the rule that generates a table of values and express it symbolically.
  • Translate a verbal relationship into an expression or equation, watching the direction of the operation.
  • Explain a step in solving an equation using a balance model.

Geometry, Measurement and Data — 20 questions (about 29%)

Classifying shapes by their defining properties, area and volume, unit conversion, angle relationships, and data — displays, measures of center, and what each measure does and does not tell you.

Typical question shapes:

  • Distinguish a perimeter computation from an area computation in a student’s work.
  • Convert between units and decide whether a stated conversion is correct.
  • Choose the measure of center that best describes a data set containing an outlier.

Teaching scenario items (approximately 10–15%)

ETS notes that the Elementary Education Fundamentals tests include 10–15% Tasks of Teaching items[5]. These embed content in a classroom situation — a student sample, a brief exchange, a lesson fragment — and ask what you would do as the teacher. They are distributed across the three domains above, not a separate section.

A note on what we are not telling you. ETS publishes the domain names and item counts above. It does not publish a full public blueprint with sub-skill-level weightings for Praxis 8003. The “typical question shapes” above are our professional reading of the domain definitions, not an ETS item list. Treat them as orientation, not as a specification.

Calculator and Numeric-Entry Questions

These two features are what make 8003 different from the reading tests in the same family, and they are where candidates lose points they did not need to lose.

What the calculator policy actually says

ETS states that an on-screen scientific calculator is provided for Praxis 8003, and recommends that candidates familiarize themselves with the calculator’s functions before test day[1][5].

That is the whole of the claim we publish, and we are deliberately not extending it:

  • We have not verified a bring-your-own-calculator option for 8003, and we do not tell you to bring one.
  • We have not verified a separate request or approval process for calculator use on this test.
  • We do not name a specific calculator model. The official page states that a scientific calculator is provided; if you want to know which interface you will see, work through the calculator training material ETS publishes and treat the official test page as the authority[1].

Any change to calculator availability appears on the official test page first. Check it before test day[1].

How to practice the calculator so it helps rather than costs. The calculator removes arithmetic load; it does not remove reasoning load, and it introduces entry errors. Practice these three habits:

  • Write the expression before you type it. Entering as you read is how parentheses get skipped and a correct method produces a wrong number.
  • Estimate before you calculate. A decimal misplacement or an order-of-operations slip is invisible on a display and obvious against an estimate.
  • Use it for what it is for. Multi-step arithmetic, awkward numbers, roots and powers, and precise decimal work. Mental math is faster for the rest and leaves time for the items that need thought.

Numeric-entry questions: producing the answer instead of recognising it

Some 8003 questions ask you to type the answer rather than choose it. Elimination is unavailable, so a numeric-entry item is lost twice as easily: a right method entered in the wrong form scores the same as a wrong method.

Use a fixed routine so the form of the answer is decided before the calculator is touched:

  1. Identify exactly what quantity the item asks for — a value, a count, a percent, an amount of money.
  2. Write the relationship or equation you need.
  3. Estimate the range the answer should fall in.
  4. Calculate.
  5. Check sign, unit, decimal placement, and reasonableness against your estimate.
  6. Enter only the requested form. If the prompt asks for a decimal, a fraction is not equivalent input; if it asks for a number only, units and percent signs do not belong in the box.

What we do not know: ETS lists the question types for 8003 as selected-response and numeric-entry but does not publish how many items are numeric-entry[1][5]. Any page that gives you an exact count without citing ETS is guessing, and we do not repeat those numbers.

How Praxis 8003 Differs from Praxis 5003

Candidates who prepared for 5003 will recognise the mathematics. The structure, the timing, and one of the domain names all change:

Praxis 5003Praxis 8003
Test formSubtest of the 5001 Multiple Subjects testStandalone test in the Elementary Education Fundamentals family[5]
Questions50[7]68[1][5]
Time65 minutes[7]100 minutes[1][5]
Third domain nameGeometry and Measurement, Data, Statistics, and Probability[7]Geometry, Measurement and Data[4][5]
CalculatorOn-screen scientific calculator provided[7]On-screen scientific calculator provided[1]
Category-level retakesNot availablePraxis Steps categories 8301 / 8302 / 8303[5]
RetirementRetires 2028-08[5]Current series

The two practical consequences: you get 35 more minutes and 18 more questions, which is a slightly more generous pace than 5003 (about 88 seconds per item against about 78), and the third domain is now named without statistics and probability in the title. The mathematics itself is not more advanced — it is elementary content tested at greater length, with more room for the reasoning items that separate a passing score from a comfortable one.

Scoring: What You Can and Cannot Predict

Praxis 8003 is scored on a 100–200 scale[6]. Your qualifying score is set by your state licensing agency or preparation program, not by ETS and not nationally[2][3]. The two verified 8003 cut scores are 152 (West Virginia) and 136 (Arkansas)[2][3].

Two claims circulate online that are wrong, and that we do not repeat:

  • That the 8000 series uses a 100–300 scale with a 240 passing score. It does not. Verified qualifying scores in the 8000 series fall in the 126–152 range on a 100–200 scale[2][3][6].
  • That there is a single national cut score for 8003. There is not. Any page that gives you one number without naming a state is guessing[2][3].

Because there is no public official conversion table from raw items to scaled score for the 8000 series, we do not convert your practice percentage into a projected scaled score, and we do not give a pass probability. A practice percentage is a practice percentage. Presenting it as a predicted scaled score would be a fabrication, and it is the kind of number people make registration decisions on.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Praxis 8003?

Praxis 8003 is Elementary Education Fundamentals: Mathematics — a 100-minute, 68-question exam covering Numbers and Operations (28 questions), Algebraic Thinking (20), and Geometry, Measurement and Data (20). Questions are selected-response and numeric-entry, and an on-screen scientific calculator is provided[1][4][5].

Is Praxis 8003 the same as Praxis 5003?

No. 5003 is the mathematics subtest of the older Elementary Education: Multiple Subjects test — 50 questions in 65 minutes[7]. 8003 is a standalone test in the redesigned Elementary Education Fundamentals family with 68 questions in 100 minutes[1][5]. The category names also changed: what 5003 called Geometry and Measurement, Data, Statistics, and Probability is now Geometry, Measurement and Data[4][5].

Can I use a calculator on Praxis 8003?

ETS states that an on-screen scientific calculator is provided for Praxis 8003, and recommends that candidates familiarize themselves with the calculator’s functions before test day[1][5]. We do not publish any claim beyond that: we have not verified a bring-your-own-calculator option or a separate accommodation process for this test, so check the official test page before you register[1].

How many numeric-entry questions are on Praxis 8003?

ETS lists the question types for 8003 as selected-response and numeric-entry but does not publish a count for either type[1][5]. We do not know how many of the 68 items are numeric-entry, and any page that gives you an exact number without citing ETS is guessing.

Which states require Praxis 8003?

We have verified qualifying scores for Praxis 8003 in two states: West Virginia (152) and Arkansas (136)[2][3]. Adoption status in the other 48 states is not verified, and we do not publish unverified adoption claims. Check https://praxis.ets.org/state-requirements/<yourstate>-tests.html and search for Elementary Education Fundamentals: Mathematics before registering.

What is the passing score for Praxis 8003?

There is no national passing score. Each state sets its own qualifying score. The two 8003 qualifying scores we have verified are 152 (West Virginia) and 136 (Arkansas)[2][3]. Your state’s ETS requirements page is the authoritative source.

Can I take the Praxis 8003 content categories separately?

ETS lists Praxis Steps for 8003 under three category codes: 8301 Numbers and Operations, 8302 Algebraic Thinking, and 8303 Geometry, Measurement and Data[5]. ETS pages disagree on when Steps starts for the 8002–8005 tests — one page says September 2026, other ETS material says earlier — so confirm current availability before you plan a category-level retake[5].

Does Learndiag convert my practice percentage into a Praxis 8003 scaled score?

No. ETS publishes no raw-to-scaled conversion table for the 8000 series, so a conversion from a practice percentage to a scaled score would be fabricated[6]. The mini test reports a percentage and a domain-level breakdown, and nothing else.

Is this Praxis 8003 practice test official?

No. Learndiag is an independent study tool, not affiliated with ETS. Every practice question is original, written to the official content category definitions published by ETS, and none are official ETS test items[1].
