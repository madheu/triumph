How the Praxis 5001 is scored

The 5001 (Elementary Education: Multiple Subjects) is not one score, it's four. Each subtest gets its own scaled score from 100 to 200:

  • 5002 Reading & Language Arts — 80 selected-response questions, 90 minutes
  • 5003 Mathematics — 50 questions, 65 minutes
  • 5004 Social Studies — 60 questions, 60 minutes
  • 5005 Science — 55 questions, 60 minutes

In most states you must pass all four, not the exam as a whole. Your target is four separate numbers, and a strong math score does not rescue a failed reading score. Your raw score is simply the number of questions you answered correctly. ETS converts it to a scaled score through equating, a statistical adjustment meant to keep scores comparable across test forms of slightly different difficulty.

Why there is no single raw-to-scaled table

Because equating shifts with every form, ETS never publishes one fixed conversion table for the 5001. The same raw count on two different test dates can land a point or two apart on the 100–200 scale. That means any converter you find, including this one, is an estimate. The honest way to use it: get a rough number, then aim comfortably above your state's line instead of right at it.

What a scaled score means (and what it doesn't)

A 157 is not "57 percent correct." The scaled score is a calibrated measurement, not a percentage, and the raw-to-scaled relationship differs by subtest. Two scores that look alike across subtests are not the same measurement: a 157 on 5003 and a 157 on 5004 both clear their lines, but the underlying raw requirements are not equal.

Your score report also shows raw points earned vs. available for each content category within a subtest. That breakdown is the best study signal on the page. Find the categories where your earned-to-available ratio is lowest, study those first, and recheck the same categories before you rebook. The national picture is covered on the subtests explained page.

How to find your state's qualifying score

  1. Start at the ETS score-requirements page. Open praxis.ets.org/scores, select your state, and search for "Elementary Education: Multiple Subjects (5001)" or the subtest codes 5002–5005. Record every number shown.
  2. Open your state's required-tests page on ETS. The state requirements directory links each state's official list of accepted tests and qualifying scores.
  3. Cross-check with your state licensing agency. State education departments publish their own certification handbooks. Search "[State] Department of Education teacher certification Praxis requirements" and confirm a .gov source, since agency rules can lag behind or differ from what ETS displays.
  4. Ask your educator preparation program. Many programs impose internal score floors stricter than the state minimum.
  5. Save the page and the date. Requirements change. Keep the official page you relied on and re-check before you register.

If your state's ETS page shows no score for the 5001, it usually means the state moved to a different elementary test, for example the 7001 series, the 7811 CKT series, or the new 8000-series Elementary Education Fundamentals tests. "No score listed" does not mean "no test required." Verify with the agency.

Do Praxis scores expire?

Keep two things apart: how long ETS will report a score, and how long your state will accept it. ETS reports a score for some years after testing, and each state decides its own acceptance window. Some accept indefinitely, others set one (Kentucky's five-year rule applies to certification on its own terms, not as a national standard). Read your state's reciprocity or score-validity guidance and whether it applies to your certificate and route before planning around an old score.

The 8000 series changes the blueprint

If your state requires the new 8000 series, the 5001 subtests no longer match: 8002–8005 have different question counts and content weights. Prep around those codes instead, and see Praxis 5001 vs. the 8000 series for the differences.

Frequently asked questions

Is 157 a passing score on Praxis 5002?

157 is the qualifying score several states, including Virginia, Tennessee, and New Jersey, currently list for 5002. It is not universal, so look up the exact number for your state and certificate.

Do wrong answers lower my score?

No. There is no guessing penalty on Praxis. An unanswered question is a guaranteed miss, so always answer every question, even if you're guessing.

If I fail one subtest, do I retake the whole 5001?

Usually not. Because each subtest is scored separately, most states let you retake just the one you failed. A high math score does not offset a failed reading score, and a few states run their own retake rules, so confirm yours before rebooking.

How accurate is this calculator?

It uses a linear estimate, scaled ≈ 100 + (raw ÷ total) × 100, and ETS's equated conversion usually lands within a few points of it. Treat it as a guide for targeting, not an official prediction.

Do Praxis scores expire?

It depends on your state. ETS reports a score for some years, then each state decides how long to accept it, and some accept it indefinitely. Check your state's reciprocity or score-validity guidance for your certificate.

Does any of this apply if my state uses the 8000 series?

Only partly. The 8002–8005 subtests have different question counts and content weights, so build your prep around the new codes. The same "state sets the line, not ETS" rule still holds.
