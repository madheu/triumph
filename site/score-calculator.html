How the Praxis 5001 is scored

The Praxis 5001 (Elementary Education: Multiple Subjects) is not one score — it's four. Each subtest (5002 Reading & Language Arts, 5003 Mathematics, 5004 Social Studies, 5005 Science) gets its own scaled score from 100 to 200, and you must pass all four to earn the license.

Your raw score is simply the number of questions you answered correctly. ETS converts it to the scaled score through equating — a statistical adjustment that keeps scores comparable across different test forms. That's why there is no single official raw-to-scaled table: it shifts a few points by form.

Roughly how many do I need to pass?

Most states set the bar between 150 and 162 per subtest — a 160 line is the most common benchmark. On a 100–200 scale, that works out to roughly 60% correct; practicing to 65%+ gives you a safety margin.

Frequently asked

Is the scaled score the same for all four subtests?

The scale is the same (100–200), but you receive four separate scores — and each must clear your state's line.

Do wrong answers hurt more than skipped ones?

No. On Praxis there is no penalty for guessing — every unanswered question is a guaranteed miss, so always guess.

Can I combine strong subtests across attempts?

No — you must pass all four subtests within a single attempt to count as passing the 5001.
