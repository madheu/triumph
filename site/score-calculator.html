How Praxis Scoring Works: Raw vs. Scaled Scores

Your raw score is simply the number of questions you answered correctly. On a selected-response Praxis test, there is no penalty for guessing, so your raw score equals your correct count. But ETS does not report raw scores to you or to your state. Instead, your raw score is converted into a scaled score, which for both the 5000 series and the new 8000 series runs on a 100–200 scale.

Why scale at all? Because different administrations of the same test use different question sets, and some forms are slightly harder than others. ETS uses a statistical process called equating to adjust the raw-to-scaled conversion for each form, so that a 152 on one form represents the same level of knowledge as a 152 on another. This is fair for test takers — but it also means no single universal raw-to-scaled table exists, even for the long-running 5000 series. The same raw score can map to slightly different scaled scores depending on the form you happened to receive.

One more wrinkle: Praxis tests may include unscored pretest questions — items ETS is trying out for future exams. These do not count toward your score, and you cannot identify them while testing. So even your "raw score" from a real sitting is an approximation of the scored portion.

Why There Is No Official 8000-Series Conversion Table (Yet)

The 8000 series — Elementary Education Fundamentals — launched with its first full administration on March 9, 2026, and score reporting began April 17, 2026. Because equating requires data from real administrations, ETS has not published any official raw-to-scaled conversion table for these tests, and the official study companions do not contain one either. Any website that shows you an exact-looking "official" conversion chart for 8002–Praxis 8006 is presenting invented numbers.

What is official: state passing scores. West Virginia's ETS state requirements page lists passing scores of 152 (8002), 152 (8003), 147 (8004), and 143 (8005); Arkansas lists 137 (8002), 136 (8003), 130 (8004), and 126 (8005). Five more states — Virginia, Idaho, South Carolina, South Dakota, and Vermont — publish confirmed 8000-series figures on their official ETS pages (all in the 143–152 band), and Idaho, South Dakota, and Vermont also list Praxis 8006 at 152. Every one of those numbers sits inside the 100–200 range, which is direct evidence for the scale itself.

Setting the record straight: the 8000 series is scored 100–200, not 100–300

You may have seen third-party prep sites claiming the 8000 series uses a 100–300 scale with a passing score of 240. That is incorrect. It directly contradicts the official state data above: West Virginia cannot require 152 and Arkansas 137 on a test scored out of 300 with a 240 passing bar. Those figures only make sense on a 100–200 scale — the same scale as the 5000 series. If a site's 8000-series score information does not match the 100–200 scale, treat everything else on that page with suspicion.

How This Estimate Is Calculated

Since no official table exists, this calculator uses a transparent linear approximation:

Estimated scaled score = 100 + (correct ÷ total questions) × 100

In plain English: your percentage correct is mapped onto the 100–200 range, so 0% correct estimates a 100, 50% correct estimates a 150, and 100% correct estimates a 200. The target-score reverse calculation runs the same line in the other direction: required percent correct = (target − 100) ÷ 100, rounded up to the next whole question.

We show you this formula openly because you deserve to know exactly where the number comes from — and exactly why it might be wrong.

Limitations: Read This Before Trusting the Number

  • Real conversions are not linear. ETS equating typically compresses the middle of the scale, which means the passing range often requires somewhat more than the linear estimate suggests. Historically, on many Praxis tests, passing has required noticeably more than half the questions correct. Plan your studying with a safety margin — aim to beat the estimated requirement, not just meet it.
  • Each form is different. The conversion for your specific test form was set after your administration and is not public.
  • Some questions may not count. Unscored pretest items mean your effective scored question count may be lower than the published total.
  • Question counts for both series are confirmed on official ETS test pages. The 5000-series totals are 5001: 245; 5002: 80; 5003: 50; 5004: 60; 5005: 55 (verified 2026-09-27), and the 8002–Praxis 8006 totals likewise come from official ETS test pages.
  • This tool is for planning, not prediction. Use it to set practice targets and measure progress across practice tests — not to forecast your official score report.

Worked Examples

Example 1 — Praxis 5001 (Multiple Subjects)

Maya takes a full-length 5001 practice battery of 245 questions across the four subtests (Reading & Language Arts, Mathematics, Social Studies, Science) and answers 160 correctly. Her estimated scaled score is:

100 + (160 ÷ 245) × 100 = 100 + 65.3 ≈ 165 Estimate

Her state's passing score is 157 (hypothetical — always check your own state's requirements). The reverse calculation says she needs (157 − 100) ÷ 100 = 57% correct, or ⌈0.57 × 245⌉ = 140 correct answers Estimate. She is already about 20 questions above the estimated bar — a comfortable cushion, and she can focus her remaining prep on her weakest subtest rather than the whole battery.

Example 2 — Praxis 8002 (Reading and Language Arts), West Virginia candidate

Jordan is a West Virginia candidate taking 8002, which has 80 questions (confirmed on the official ETS test page). On a timed practice test, Jordan answers 46 correctly. Estimated scaled score:

100 + (46 ÷ 80) × 100 = 100 + 57.5 ≈ 158 Estimate

West Virginia's passing score for 8002 is 152 (confirmed via the ETS state requirements page). To reach it, Jordan needs an estimated (152 − 100) ÷ 100 = 52% correct, which is ⌈0.52 × 80⌉ = 42 correct answers Estimate. Jordan's 46 clears that by 4 questions — but remember the limitation above: real equating is not linear, so a 4-question cushion is thin. Jordan should keep practicing until consistently scoring 50+ correct before test day.

FAQ: 5003 scoring

Is the 5003 scored separately from the rest of the 5001?

Yes. Each 5001 subtest (5002, 5003, 5004, 5005) receives its own scaled score on the 100–200 scale, and states set passing scores per subtest. A strong 5003 score cannot offset a weak 5005 score.

How many questions can I miss on the 5003 and still pass?

It depends on your state's passing score and on the specific form you receive. Use the target-score mode above: select 5003, enter your state's passing score, and the calculator estimates how many correct answers you need out of 50. Treat the result as a planning estimate and build in a safety margin above the passing line.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this an official ETS Praxis score calculator?

No. LearnDiag is not affiliated with or endorsed by ETS. ETS does not publish raw-to-scaled conversion tables for the 8000 series, and 5000-series conversions vary by test form. Every number this tool produces is a clearly labeled Estimate.

How accurate is the linear estimate?

It is a reasonable planning approximation but not a prediction. Real equating is non-linear and form-specific, and the true passing bar often sits somewhat above the linear midpoint. Treat the estimate as a floor to beat with margin, especially within ±10 scaled points of your target.

What scaled score do I need to pass?

Passing scores are set by each state, not by ETS. For the 8000 series, seven states publish confirmed figures on their official ETS state requirements pages: West Virginia (143–152 by subject), Arkansas (126–137), Virginia (143–152), and Idaho, South Carolina, South Dakota, and Vermont (143–152 where listed). For all other states and for 5000-series passing scores, check your state's page on the official ETS state requirements site.

Do all questions on the test count toward my score?

Not necessarily. Praxis tests may include unscored pretest questions used to develop future forms. You cannot identify them, so your raw practice count is always an approximation of the scored portion.

Do I get a calculator on Praxis 8003 or 8005?

Yes — both 8003 (Mathematics) and 8005 (Science) provide an on-screen scientific calculator during the test. The other 8000-series tests do not offer one. Practice with an on-screen calculator before test day so the interface does not slow you down.

How many questions are on Praxis 8005, and what do they cover?

Praxis 8005 (Science) has 74 questions over 90 minutes — the only one of the five 8000-series tests timed at 90 minutes instead of 100. The official ETS Study Companion (Elementary-Education-V5.pdf, p.4) confirms the domain breakdown: Earth and Space Sciences 24 questions (32%), Life Science 25 (34%), and Physical Science 25 (34%), which sum to the published total of 74.

Is Praxis 8006 the same as PECT 8006?

No. Praxis 8006 (Elementary Education Fundamentals: Teaching Reading) is an ETS test. PECT PreK-4 Module 1 — also numbered 8006 — is a Pennsylvania certification test made by Pearson, covering child development and assessment. They share a code number by coincidence and are completely unrelated. Note also that Idaho, South Dakota, and Vermont list Praxis 8006 at a qualifying score of 152, and its Praxis Steps rollout is scheduled for Spring 2027 (ETS has not announced a specific month), later than the September 2026 rollout for 8002–8005.

When will an official 8000-series conversion table exist?

ETS has not announced one. Conversion information typically remains internal even for established tests; your official score report is the only authoritative conversion of your performance. We will update this page if ETS publishes official conversion data.
