 Facts last verified: September 27, 2026.   How this planner decides your schedule The plan is a starting schedule, not a prescription. Here is exactly what it uses and what it ignores, so you can decide whether to follow it.  **It uses your test date and your practice results by subtest.** More days get allocated to the subtest where your accuracy is lowest, because that is the gate most likely to fail you. On Praxis 5001 the four subtests are scored separately — you do not pass on an average. **It uses your reported study hours per day.** Change that number and the daily blocks resize. **It does not know your state.** The planner has no qualifying-score data, so it cannot tell you how close you are to a line. Look yours up on the [passing scores by state](https://learndiag.com/praxis-5001-passing-score-by-state) page. **It does not predict your score.** We weight by your weakest subtest because that is a structural fact about the exam, not because we can forecast your result. Nothing in the plan is derived from a predicted scaled score. **The 14-day length is our choice, not ETS's.** ETS publishes no recommended study duration.  Exam structure and scoring reflect [ETS's Praxis 5001 test page](https://praxis.ets.org/test/elementary-education-multiple-subjects-subtests-5001.html) and the [ETS score scale page](https://praxis.ets.org/understanding-scores.html), checked October 2026.
