# Praxis 8002 Reading and Language Arts: What the Test Covers and How to Prepare

*2026-09-15 · Facts last verified 2026-09-03 · Reading time: about 10 min*

**Test requirements vary by state, preparation program, and effective date. Confirm your required test code with your state licensing agency or educator preparation program before registering.**

**Praxis 8002** — officially *Elementary Education Fundamentals: Reading and Language Arts* — is the reading and language arts test in ETS’s redesigned Elementary Education Fundamentals family. It is a 100-minute, 80-question selected-response test with no calculator, first administered on 2026-03-09[5]. It is one of four core subject tests in the family, alongside 8003 Mathematics, 8004 Social Studies, and 8005 Science[1].

Still Preparing for the Legacy 5002?  ETS targets August 2028 for retirement of the legacy series, including 5002. Verify current appointment availability and state acceptance separately. If your state or program requires the 5002 rather than Praxis 8002, use our [Praxis 5002 study guide](https://learndiag.com/praxis-5002-study-guide) — it covers the legacy exam's reading and language arts content, test prep strategy, and how the two tests relate. Always confirm which test code your state accepts on the official state requirements page before registering.

**Praxis 8002 measures your own reading and writing.** The questions ask you to read a short text and identify its central idea, or to choose the sentence with correct subject-verb agreement. Praxis 8006, by contrast, measures whether you can *teach* reading — the Science of Reading, the five components of reading instruction, and instructional decision-making. If those two sentences do not yet sound different to you, read [How Praxis 8002 Differs from Praxis 8006](https://learndiag.com/#differs-from-8006) before you register for anything.

This page explains what the test covers, who it is for, how it differs from both 8006 and the older 5002 subtest, and how to confirm which code your state actually requires. It also includes a free 30-question practice mini test with explanations. For the family as a whole, see [the Praxis Elementary Education Fundamentals hub](https://learndiag.com/praxis-elementary-education-fundamentals).

## Is Praxis 8002 the Right Test for You?

Candidates who need Praxis 8002 are typically in one of these situations:

- Their state requires the reading and language arts test in the Elementary Education Fundamentals family for elementary licensure, and has adopted 8002 for that purpose.
- Their educator preparation program requires a reading and language arts content test as a program completion or exit requirement.
- They are certifying in a state that accepts the 8000 series in place of the older 5000 series, and 5002 would have been the test they took.

**What we can verify, and what we cannot.** We have verified the status of Praxis 8002 in two states. In both, 8002 is the test in use:

| State | Status of Praxis 8002 | Qualifying score |
| --- | --- | --- |
| West Virginia | Adopted | 152[2] |
| Arkansas | Adopted | 137[3] |

Both of those states adopted 8002 *without* adopting 8006 — West Virginia uses Praxis 5205 for teaching reading instead[2]. That is a useful reminder that the two tests travel separately: a state adopting the 8000 series for reading and language arts says nothing about whether it also adopted the teaching reading test.

For the remaining 48 states, adoption status is **not verified**. We do not publish adoption claims we have not confirmed against a state agency or ETS state requirements page, because a wrong answer here costs you a test fee and a testing window.

**How to confirm your test code in about five minutes:**

1. Open your state’s ETS requirements page: `https://praxis.ets.org/state-requirements/<state>-tests.html`. (State names are written as one word, no hyphen — `westvirginia`, not `west-virginia`.)
2. Search the page for **Reading and Language Arts**. Note the code and the qualifying score.
3. If the page lists both an old and a new code, contact your educator preparation program or state licensing agency and ask which code applies to your certification pathway.

**Numbers you may see elsewhere that are wrong.** At least one commercial prep site advertises Praxis 8002 as 55 questions, 1 hour 15 minutes, $130, with a passing score of 157. Every one of those figures contradicts the ETS test page and the ETS state requirements pages[1][2][3]. The verified facts are 80 questions, 100 minutes, $79.00, and a state-set qualifying score (152 in West Virginia, 137 in Arkansas). If a page presents those invented figures as an official blueprint, treat everything else on that page as unverified too.

## What Praxis 8002 Actually Tests

ETS publishes two content categories with fixed item counts for Praxis 8002[4]. Both are about your command of the English language and of written texts — not about classroom methodology. That distinction is the whole point of the test, and it is why 8002 and 8006 are not interchangeable.

### Reading — 42 questions (52%)

The larger category. It covers what a competent adult reader does with a text: locating central ideas and supporting details, drawing inferences that the text actually supports, recognizing how a text is organized, identifying an author’s purpose, interpreting figurative language, comparing two texts that address the same question, and working out the meaning of unfamiliar words from context, word parts, and the structure of English spelling and syllables[4].

Typical question shapes:

- Read a short paragraph and choose the sentence that best states its central idea.
- Choose the inference that the details in a paragraph actually support.
- Identify the meaning a multiple-meaning word carries in a given sentence.
- Explain why a figurative expression cannot be read literally.

### Writing, Speaking and Listening — 38 questions (48%)

The smaller category, and the one candidates most often under-prepare because it looks like ordinary English. It covers standard written English conventions — subject-verb agreement, verb tense, pronoun reference, sentence boundaries such as comma splices and run-ons, punctuation including the semicolon, possessive apostrophes, and spelling patterns — together with composition skills: distinguishing revision from editing, organizing an argument, choosing precise and concise wording, evaluating whether a source is credible, and adjusting language for an audience[4].

Typical question shapes:

- Choose the sentence in which subject and verb agree.
- Identify which of several sentences uses a semicolon correctly.
- Select the most concise revision that preserves the original meaning.
- Decide which action fixes a draft whose problem is content rather than mechanics.

### Tasks of Teaching (approximately 10–15%)

ETS notes that the Elementary Education Fundamentals tests include approximately 10–15% Tasks of Teaching items[5]. These embed a content question inside a classroom or professional situation — a draft that has gone off topic, a discussion in which one participant restates a colleague’s point — and they are distributed across both categories rather than forming a separate section. Note what they are *not*: they do not ask you to design reading instruction. That is Praxis 8006.

**A note on what we are not telling you.** ETS publishes the category names and item counts above. It does not publish a public sub-skill blueprint for Praxis 8002, and it does not release a pool of sample items. The “typical question shapes” above are our professional reading of the category definitions, not an ETS item list. Treat them as orientation, not as a specification.

## How Praxis 8002 Differs from Praxis 8006

This is the most common point of confusion in the whole 8000 series, and it is worth slowing down for. Both tests are in the Elementary Education Fundamentals family. Both cost $79. Both take 100 minutes and ask 80 selected-response questions. Both have the word “Reading” in the official name. And they are not the same test at all.

**The short version.** Praxis 8002 asks whether *you* can read and write well enough to teach elementary school. Praxis 8006 asks whether you know how to *teach* reading. One is a test of your literacy; the other is a test of your reading pedagogy.

|  | Praxis 8002 | Praxis 8006 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Official name** | Elementary Education Fundamentals: Reading and Language Arts | Elementary Education Fundamentals: Teaching Reading |
| **What it measures** | Your own reading, writing, speaking and listening | Your knowledge of how reading is taught |
| **Content basis** | Literary and informational text, vocabulary and word analysis, conventions, composition | Science of Reading; the five components of reading instruction[7] |
| **Content categories** | Reading (42) · Writing, Speaking and Listening (38)[4] | Foundational Literacy Skills (32) · Fluency and Vocabulary (24) · Comprehension and Written Expression (24)[4] |
| **Role in the family** | One of four core subject tests | The specialized reading pedagogy test |
| **Verified state adoption** | West Virginia (152) · Arkansas (137)[2][3] | Neither — West Virginia uses Praxis 5205[2] |

**Why they get mixed up.** Three reasons, in order of how often we see them:

1. **The names.** “Reading and Language Arts” and “Teaching Reading” read like two labels for one subject. They are not: one names the subject matter, the other names the act of teaching it.
2. **The codes.** 8002 and 8006 sit four digits apart in the same family and share the same fee, format, and launch date, which makes them look like variants of one test rather than two different tests.
3. **The word “Reading” inside 8002.** Because 42 of 8002’s 80 questions are in a category called Reading, candidates assume it must cover phonics and reading instruction. It does not. Those 42 questions test your comprehension, vocabulary, and word analysis as a reader, not your ability to run a phonics lesson.

**How to decide.** Ask what your state or program names as the requirement, then match the code — never match the subject word.

- If the requirement is a reading and language arts *content* test for elementary certification in the 8000 series, that is 8002.
- If the requirement is a *teaching reading* exam — sometimes for an elementary license, sometimes for a reading endorsement or reading specialist pathway — that is 8006, or an alternative such as Praxis 5205.
- If a state or program page shows both, they are separate requirements rather than options, and asking your preparation program which one applies to your pathway is faster than guessing.

We cover the other side of this comparison on our [Praxis 8006 Teaching Reading guide](https://learndiag.com/praxis-8006-teaching-reading), which is written for candidates who need the pedagogy exam.

## How Praxis 8002 Differs from Praxis 5002

Candidates who prepared for Praxis 5002 will recognize most of the content. The structural difference is larger than the content difference, and the relationship is not a straightforward replacement.

**Praxis 5002 is a subtest; Praxis 8002 is a test.** 5002 is the reading and language arts subtest of Praxis 5001 (Elementary Education: Multiple Subjects), which candidates typically schedule as a set of four subtests. The 8000 series has no equivalent all-in-one option: ETS states that the Elementary Education Fundamentals family consists of separate subject tests, with no combined test[1]. So 8002 is not “5002 with a new number” — it is the same subject matter in a different structure.

|  | Praxis 5002 | Praxis 8002 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Official name** | Elementary Education: Reading and Language Arts Subtest | Elementary Education Fundamentals: Reading and Language Arts |
| **Family** | Praxis 5001 (Multiple Subjects), four subtests | Elementary Education Fundamentals, separate tests, no combined option[1] |
| **Questions** | 80 | 80 |
| **Time** | 90 minutes | 100 minutes[5] |
| **Fee** | $64 | $79.00[1] |
| **Categories** | Reading (38) · Writing, Speaking, and Listening (42) | Reading (42) · Writing, Speaking and Listening (38)[4] |
| **Balance** | Writing and language slightly larger | Reading larger — about 52% against about 48% |
| **Category-level retakes** | Not available | Praxis Steps 8201 and 8202[2][5] |

**Where the 5002 column comes from.** Those figures are ETS’s published 5002 test page and the guidance our own [Praxis 5002 study guide](https://learndiag.com/praxis-5002-study-guide) is built on[8] — not from the 8000-series facts library, which does not cover the legacy series. If you are still registered for 5002, confirm the current fee and time on the 5002 page before your test date.

Two details are worth noting because they trip people up. First, the category balance flips: in 5002 the writing and listening category is the larger one, while in 8002 Reading is larger. Second, the official name of the second category is punctuated differently between the two tests — 5002 uses *Writing, Speaking, and Listening* with a serial comma, 8002 uses *Writing, Speaking and Listening* without one[4]. That is not a typo on either page; it is how each test’s category is published, and we reproduce both exactly.

**Which one you take depends on your state and its timing.** The legacy series — 5001, 7001, 5901 and 7811 — has a target retirement date of 2028-08[5], and states that have adopted the 8000 series set their own acceptance windows. As one verified example, Arkansas accepts 5000-series results through 2027-08-31 and honours those scores until 2031-08-31, with 5000 and 8000 results usable in combination before that date provided every required area is covered by at least one passing test[3]. If you are partway through the 5001 series, ask your state agency how it treats a mixed record before you switch. Our [5001 vs 8000 series comparison](https://learndiag.com/praxis-5001-vs-8000-series) and the [Praxis 5002 study guide](https://learndiag.com/praxis-5002-study-guide) cover that ground in more detail.

### Which study plan fits which weakness

The mini test result tells you which of the two categories to work on first. It does not tell you what to do about it, so here is the mapping we would use.

- **Reading is weakest.** Your gap is comprehension and vocabulary, so practice the routines that produce it: read a paragraph and write its central idea in one sentence, then check whether every sentence you wrote appears in the text. Add vocabulary work through context clues and morphology rather than word lists.
- **Writing, Speaking and Listening is weakest.** Your gap is usually conventions, not ideas. Drill the specific rules the mini test exposes — subject-verb agreement with intervening phrases, verb-tense consistency in a series, comma splices and run-ons, semicolon versus comma before a conjunction, possessive apostrophes — and read each explanation for the rule you missed rather than the option you missed.
- **“Which TWO” items are where you lose points.** These punish one specific habit: stopping at the first option that looks right. Judge all five options independently before you select, and treat each one as a separate true/false judgment.
- **Both categories are close and both are low.** Start with Reading, because it carries 42 of the 80 items — the larger share of the score is there.
- **One category is strong and the other is not.** Work the weak one. Improving a category you already score well in moves the total less than repairing a collapse.

A percentage on a practice test is a percentage on a practice test. ETS publishes no conversion table from raw items to scaled score for the 8000 series, so we do not translate your practice result into a predicted scaled score, and nobody else can either[6]. Treat it as a map of where to study next. The wider family is covered on [the 8000-series hub](https://learndiag.com/praxis-elementary-education-fundamentals).

## Scoring: What You Can and Cannot Predict

Praxis 8002 is scored on a 100–200 scale[6]. Your qualifying score is set by your state licensing agency or preparation program, not by ETS and not nationally[2][3]. Among the states we have verified, West Virginia requires 152 and Arkansas requires 137[2][3].

Two claims circulate online that are wrong, and that we do not repeat:

- **That the 8000 series uses a 100-300 scale with a 240 passing score.** It does not. Verified qualifying scores in the 8000 series fall in the 126–152 range on a 100–200 scale[2][3][6].
- **That there is a single national cut score for 8002.** There is not. Any page that gives you one number without naming a state is guessing[2][3].

Because there is no public official conversion table from raw items to scaled score for Praxis 8002, **we do not offer a scaled score calculator for this test.** A practice percentage is a practice percentage. Presenting it as a predicted scaled score would be a fabrication, and it is the kind of number people make registration decisions on.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Praxis 8002?

Praxis 8002 is Elementary Education Fundamentals: Reading and Language Arts, a 100-minute, 80-question selected-response exam with no calculator. It is one of the four core subject tests in ETS’s Elementary Education Fundamentals family and was first administered on 2026-03-09[1][5].

### What is the difference between Praxis 8002 and Praxis 8006?

8002 measures your own reading, writing, speaking and listening. 8006 measures whether you can teach reading — the Science of Reading and the five components of reading instruction. Both are 100 minutes, 80 selected-response questions, and $79[1], which is exactly why they get confused. They are separate requirements, not substitutes, and a state can adopt one without the other[2].

### Is Praxis 8002 the same as Praxis 5002?

No. 5002 is the reading and language arts subtest of Praxis 5001 (Multiple Subjects), which candidates usually take as part of a four-subtest set. 8002 is a standalone test in the Elementary Education Fundamentals family, and ETS states that family has no combined all-in-one option[1]. The content overlaps heavily, but the structure, the time (100 versus 90 minutes), the fee ($79 versus $64), the category balance, and the category-level retake options all differ.

### Which states require Praxis 8002?

We have verified two: West Virginia (qualifying score 152) and Arkansas (137)[2][3]. Adoption status in the other 48 states is not verified, and we do not publish unverified adoption claims. Check https://praxis.ets.org/state-requirements/<yourstate>-tests.html before registering.

### What is the passing score for Praxis 8002?

There is no national passing score. Each state sets its own qualifying score. The two verified figures are 152 in West Virginia and 137 in Arkansas[2][3]. Your state’s ETS requirements page is the authoritative source.

### Can I take the two content categories separately?

Yes, in principle: Praxis Steps codes 8201 (Reading) and 8202 (Writing, Speaking, and Listening) are listed for 8002 on the West Virginia state requirements page[2]. Steps allows category-level testing and retaking, so a single weak category would no longer mean retaking all 80 questions. One caveat: ETS publishes conflicting launch windows for Steps on 8002–8005 — its brochure says Spring 2026, another document says Summer 2026, and the test pages say September 2026[5]. Confirm availability for your state and test date before you plan around modular retakes.

### How many questions do I need to get right to pass?

We cannot tell you, and neither can anyone else with current data. ETS does not publish a raw-to-scaled conversion for the 8000 series, so the number of correct items that produces a given scaled score is not public[6]. A practice percentage is a readiness signal, not a score prediction.

### When are scores released?

Score reporting for the Elementary Education Fundamentals tests began 2026-04-17[5]. Reporting dates are specific to each administration window, so check the official test page for your test date[1].

### Is this free practice test official?

No. Learndiag is an independent study tool, not affiliated with ETS. Practice questions are original, written to the official content category definitions published by ETS, and are not official ETS test items[1].
