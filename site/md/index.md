Description Cause and effect Problem and solution Sequence **Answer: Description.** A descriptive structure presents a topic and then lists characteristics, features, or supporting details. Cause and effect shows relationships between events, while sequence organizes events in time order.    5003 · Mathematics A student explains that 0.5 is greater than 0.35 because 5 is greater than 35. Which representation best addresses this misconception?  Convert both numbers to fractions without visual support Show that 0.35 has more digits than 0.5 Tell the student to memorize that 0.5 is larger Use base-ten blocks to model 0.5 as 5 tenths and 0.35 as 3 tenths and 5 hundredths **Answer: the base-ten blocks.** Base-ten blocks make the place value explicit: 0.5 fills 5 of 10 columns while 0.35 fills 3 full columns plus 5 small squares. The digit-count argument reinforces the misconception. Memorization does not build understanding. Fraction conversion without visualization does not address the root confusion.    5005 · Science Students believe that seasons are caused by Earth's distance from the Sun. Which demonstration best corrects this misconception?  Draw a diagram showing a circular orbit Have students measure temperature at noon only Show that Earth's axis tilt causes different angles of sunlight on hemispheres Tell students the belief is wrong **Answer: the axis-tilt demonstration.** Demonstrating axial tilt with a globe and light source shows how the tilted axis causes direct vs. angled sunlight, creating seasons. Correcting without evidence reinforces the misconception. Measuring at noon only ignores seasonal variation. A circular orbit diagram does not address the actual cause.  Not sure where you stand? The full question bank and the 12-question readiness diagnostic are free with no signup required. Logging in at the end of the diagnostic is optional and unlocks your full report and study plan.   What is on the Praxis 5001? The Praxis 5001 (Elementary Education: Multiple Subjects) is four separately scored subtests: 5002 Reading & Language Arts, 5003 Mathematics, 5004 Social Studies, and 5005 Science. You must pass all four.   How long does the Praxis 5001 diagnostic take? About eight minutes. The diagnostic draws 12 original questions at random — 3 from each of the four subtests.   Does the diagnostic tell me my weakest subtest? Yes. It estimates a scaled score for each subtest, compares it to state passing lines, and identifies the gate most likely to fail you so you know where to study.   What score do I need to pass the Praxis 5001? Passing lines are set by each state, not by ETS, and they differ per subtest — most states require between 139 and 159 on each of the four gates. The [Praxis 5001 passing scores by state](https://learndiag.com/praxis-5001-passing-score-by-state) table lists the exact qualifying scores for 5002, 5003, 5004, and 5005 and tells you which states use the exam.   What is the Praxis 8000 series? ETS has introduced an 8000-series elementary education lineup (8002–8006), first administered March 9, 2026, with the modular Praxis Steps option available from September 2026. Which exam your state requires — the 5001 or an 8000-series test — is decided by your state, not by ETS. Compare them side by side in [Praxis 5001 vs the 8000 Series](https://learndiag.com/praxis-5001-vs-8000-series), or check the [state-by-state 8000-series requirements tracker](https://learndiag.com/praxis-8000-series-state-requirements).   How is this different from grinding random practice questions? Volume alone doesn't raise a score when the questions don't match the blueprint. Every Learndiag item is original, tagged by subtest category, and explained — start with the [free practice questions](https://learndiag.com/practice). The diagnostic then converts your pattern of answers into an estimated scaled score per subtest, your weakest gate, and a plan that puts study time where the gap is.   How is the estimated score calculated? Each of the 12 questions maps to a subtest category. Your accuracy per subtest is converted into an estimated scaled score on the 100–200 scale ETS reports, and the gap between that estimate and your state's passing line becomes your readiness signal and study plan.
