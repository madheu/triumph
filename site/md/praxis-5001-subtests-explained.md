# Praxis 5001 Subtests Explained: What's on 5002, 5003, 5004, and 5005

*2026-08-25 · Reading time: about 6 min*

The Praxis 5001 — officially *Elementary Education: Multiple Subjects* — is not one exam but **four separate subtests**, each timed, scored, and reported on its own.[1] You pass the 5001 by passing every subtest your state requires, which is why understanding what each one contains is the first step of any serious study plan.

Here is each subtest explained: what it covers, how many questions it has, how long it lasts, and how scoring works.

## The Four Praxis 5001 Subtests at a Glance

| Code | Subtest | Questions | Time | Question types |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5002 | Reading & Language Arts | 80 | 90 minutes | Selected response |
| 5003 | Mathematics | 50 | 65 minutes | Selected response + numeric entry |
| 5004 | Social Studies | 60 | 60 minutes | Selected response |
| 5005 | Science | 55 | 60 minutes | Selected response |

Together that is roughly **245 questions across about four and a half hours** if you sit all four at once. Most states let you register for subtests individually, so many candidates split them across separate test appointments.

## 5002: Reading & Language Arts (80 Questions, 90 Minutes)

The longest subtest measures what an entry-level elementary teacher must know about *teaching* literacy — not just how well you read yourself. Per the official Study Companion blueprint it splits into two categories:

- **Reading — about 38 questions (~47%)**: phonemic and phonological awareness, phonics and word recognition, vocabulary, and comprehension development.
- **Writing, Speaking, and Listening — about 42 questions (~53%)**: the writing process, conventions of standard English, and instructional practice for building students' writing and oral language.

What surprises candidates: a large share of items are pedagogy. You will be asked which instructional move or assessment fits a described student scenario, not merely to define a term. For depth, see the [Praxis 5002 study guide](https://learndiag.com/praxis-5002-study-guide).

## 5003: Mathematics (50 Questions, 65 Minutes)

Three content categories:

- **Numbers and Operations — 20 questions (40%)**: place value, fractions, decimals, percents, ratios, and operations.
- **Algebraic Thinking — 15 questions (30%)**: patterns, expressions, equations, and proportional reasoning.
- **Geometry, Measurement, Data, Statistics, and Probability — 15 questions (30%)**: shapes, measurement, data displays, and basic probability.

Two features make 5003 distinctive. First, it includes **numeric-entry questions** where you type the answer instead of choosing from options — no answer choices to eliminate. Second, an on-screen calculator is available, but relying on it for elementary arithmetic wastes time; fluency matters on a 65-minute clock. A meaningful fraction of items are mathematics *pedagogy*: diagnosing a student's misconception rather than computing. The [Praxis 5003 math study guide](https://learndiag.com/praxis-5003-math-study-guide) breaks down preparation and calculator strategy.

## 5004: Social Studies (60 Questions, 60 Minutes)

The tightest pacing of the four: sixty questions in sixty minutes. Categories:

- **United States History, Government, and Citizenship — 27 questions (45%)**: colonial era through today, the Constitution, branches of government, civic life.
- **Geography, Anthropology, and Sociology — 18 questions (30%)**: physical and human geography, culture, human–environment interaction.
- **World History and Economics — 15 questions (25%)**: major world civilizations and core economic concepts like opportunity cost and markets.

The challenge is breadth under time pressure — US history alone spans five centuries — plus scenario items about teaching social studies content. See the [Praxis 5004 social studies study guide](https://learndiag.com/praxis-5004-social-studies-study-guide) for a high-yield review plan.

## 5005: Science (55 Questions, 60 Minutes)

Science divides nearly evenly across three domains:

- **Earth Science (~33%)**: weather and the water cycle, rocks and soil, astronomy.
- **Life Science (~33%)**: cells, organisms, ecosystems.
- **Physical Science (~33%)**: matter, energy, forces, motion.

Expect **science inquiry and pedagogy** items as well — designing a fair test, controlling variables, interpreting observations. Community candidate reports frequently flag science among the hardest gates, largely because the knowledge surface is wide and most adults have not reviewed it in years. The [Praxis 5005 science study guide](https://learndiag.com/praxis-5005-science-study-guide) covers all three domains plus inquiry.

## How Praxis 5001 Scoring Works

Every subtest produces its own score, typically reported on a **100–200 scale**.[1]

- Your state sets a qualifying score for each subtest — commonly between 150 and 165, and there is no national passing standard.[1] Check [Praxis 5001 passing scores by state](https://learndiag.com/praxis-5001-passing-score-by-state) for verified numbers and the lookup method.
- You need to clear every required subtest. One failed gate fails the battery, no matter how strong the other three are.
- If you fail one subtest, you generally retake only that one — after a wait of at least 28 days and paying the subtest fee again. The [retake guide](https://learndiag.com/praxis-5001-retake-guide) explains rules, costs, and timing.

## Can You Take the Subtests Separately?

Yes — the four subtests can be scheduled individually, and taking them one or two at a time is a common strategy that keeps each appointment under two hours. Check your state's rules first, though: states differ on whether they accept subtests taken separately, the full battery, or only specific codes. The [state requirements directory](https://praxis.ets.org/state-requirements.html) lists what each state accepts.[3]

Note that the 5001 family itself is being replaced by the 8000-series Elementary Education Fundamentals tests launched in spring 2026,[2] and states are moving at different speeds — verify the code before you book.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many subtests are on the Praxis 5001?

Four: 5002 Reading & Language Arts, 5003 Mathematics, 5004 Social Studies, and 5005 Science.

### How long is the whole Praxis 5001?

About four hours thirty-five minutes of testing across the four subtests (90 + 65 + 60 + 60 minutes), plus administrative time at the test center.

### Which Praxis 5001 subtest is hardest?

It varies by background, but candidate communities most often name 5005 Science (wide knowledge surface) and 5003 Mathematics (numeric entry plus years-rusty skills). The reliable way to know yours is a diagnostic baseline, not forum consensus.

### Are all four subtests required?

In most states that accept the 5001, yes — every subtest carries its own qualifying score requirement.[1] Some states accept alternatives such as the 5901 bundle or CKT series instead.

## The Bottom Line

Four separately scored exams, four separate state-set pass lines, and one rule that shapes everything: your weakest subtest decides whether you certify. Learn each gate's blueprint, find your weakest one early, and aim above your state's line with margin.
