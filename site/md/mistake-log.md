Why a mistake log beats memorizing questions Most Praxis 5001 candidates review by rereading explanations and redoing the same questions — which feels productive but rarely moves a scaled score. The reason: recognition memory is not the same as method memory. A question you have seen once is easy the second time, but the underlying skill — for example, unpacking a two-step math word problem or spotting the teaching move a reading scenario is really testing — stays unmastered unless you name what went wrong. This log forces the naming step. Every entry classifies the miss into one of three root causes: **A — Careless** (a calculation slip or misread option, fixed by slowing down and double-checking), **B — Knowledge Gap** (a concept or formula you have not mastered, fixed by studying that blueprint category), and **C — Method Error** (the right idea, wrong procedure — fixed by rehearsing the correct steps). Then you write the method you will use next time, not the answer. When your log fills in, patterns appear: if most entries are B, your study time belongs in the [question bank](https://learndiag.com/practice) drilling that subtest; if they are mostly A, timed [diagnostic practice](https://learndiag.com/diagnostic) with a checklist matters more.
