Why you can't just trust one article (including this one) Three structural reasons make single-source answers unreliable right now.   1 Two series overlap The legacy series (5001, 7001, 5901, 7811) retires in **August 2028**. The new 8000 series began testing on **2026-03-09**. Which one applies to you depends on your state, your program, and your test date — all three.   2 Most articles are stale Much of what ranks in search was written before the 8000 series launched and still describes only the 5001. Some third-party pages even publish incorrect score scales for the new series. Anything not dated and not sourced to an official page should be treated as background reading only.   3 Codes collide **Praxis 8006** (ETS, Teaching Reading) and **PECT 8006** (Pearson, a Pennsylvania PreK-4 module) are completely different tests that share a number. The four-step verification path, summarized. Every route ends at an official source — not at this page.    Open the ETS state requirements page for your state ETS maintains per-state listings of required Praxis tests and qualifying scores. This is the master cross-check for any code you've been shown. **What to compare:** the exact test code, the exact test name, and the qualifying score. If the 8000 series appears, it will be listed by subject (8002–8006). If only legacy codes appear, note that requirements can change — check the page date.   Cross-check your state department of education The state's own certification/licensure pages are the authority ETS pages reflect. Search the department site for "teacher certification testing requirements" for your grade band. **What to compare:** which tests the state accepts for your specific license area, and any published transition or sunset dates between old and new series.   Check your educator preparation program Your program's handbook, advising page, or certification officer can specify which code they recommend for — and programs sometimes run their own transition timelines. **What to compare:** the code your program names versus what the state lists; ask specifically whether Praxis Steps are accepted if you're considering them. If the two sources disagree, that disagreement is exactly what to raise with your advisor.   Match everything against your test date The same code can be valid for one test date and retired for another. Line your planned date up against the series dates before registering. **What to compare:** first 8000-series administration (2026-03-09), Praxis Steps availability (8002–8005 from September 2026; Praxis 8006 in 2027), and the legacy-series retirement in August 2028. Frequently asked questions  Is Praxis 8006 the same as PECT 8006?  **No. They are completely unrelated tests that happen to share a number.**   Praxis 8006  Full nameElementary Education Fundamentals: Teaching Reading PublisherETS ContextNew 8000 series; state adoption in progress Scale100–200, qualifying score set by each state    PECT 8006  Full namePreK-4 Module 1: Child Development, Learning & Assessments; Collaboration & Professionalism PublisherPearson ContextPennsylvania PreK-4 certification only NotePA codes 8001/8002/8003 are likewise PAPA modules, not Praxis    Search results for "8006" mix both. If your materials say 8006, confirm which publisher's account you're registering in before anything else.    An article said the 8000 series is scored 100–300 with a passing score of 240. Is that right?  No. The 8000 series uses a **100–200** score scale, consistent with the official ETS state requirements data confirmed so far (for example, West Virginia lists qualifying scores of 152, 152, 147, and 143 for 8002–8005; Arkansas lists 137, 136, 130, 126). A "240 passing score" claim contradicts those official listings and should be treated as an error. Always read qualifying scores off the ETS state requirements page for your own state.    Can I still take the 5001 now that the 8000 series exists?  The legacy series (5001, 7001, 5901, 7811) is scheduled to retire in **August 2028**, and the two series overlap until then. But "still offered" is not the same as "accepted for your license." Whether your state and your program accept a legacy-series score for your planned date is precisely what the verification path above helps you confirm with official sources.    Will this tool tell me which test I have to take?  No — deliberately. Determining your required test is the role of your state licensing agency and your educator preparation program, and a website that answers it for you is taking on liability it can't back. What this tool does is narrower and more useful: it tells you *which official pages to open*, *which fields to compare*, and *which dates decide between series*, personalized to your inputs.    My program handbook says one code; the state page says another. Which one wins?  We won't adjudicate that — but the conflict itself is your answer: do not register until it's resolved. Contact your program's certification officer with the state page in hand, and if needed confirm with the state licensing agency directly. Discrepancies during a transition window are common; registering on an unresolved conflict is how candidates end up paying for the wrong test.    What are Praxis Steps, and should I consider them?  Praxis Steps is a staged format for the 8000 series: the content of a full test is split into separately scheduled, separately priced steps ($39.50 per step at verification time; a full single test is $79). Steps for 8002–8005 became available in September 2026; Steps for Praxis 8006 follow in 2027. Whether your state or program *accepts* Steps-based scores has not been verified for any state — treat acceptance as an open question to confirm before choosing that route.
