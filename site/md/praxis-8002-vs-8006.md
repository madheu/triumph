Both exams live in the Praxis 8000 series (Elementary Education Fundamentals) and both involve reading — but they test completely different things.   PRAXIS 8002 · READING AND LANGUAGE ARTS "Can *you* read?" Tests **your own** reading, writing, speaking & listening skills.   PRAXIS 8006 · TEACHING READING "Can you *teach someone* to read?" Tests **professional knowledge of reading instruction** — phonics, structured literacy, and reading-difficulty identification.     The one-line distinction, as a shareable card. Frequently asked questions  Is Praxis 8006 the same as PECT 8006?  **No.** They share a code number by coincidence and are otherwise unrelated. **Praxis 8006** (Elementary Education Fundamentals: Teaching Reading) is an **ETS** exam about teaching reading. **PECT 8006** (PreK-4 Module 1: Child Development, Learning & Assessments; Collaboration & Professionalism) is a **Pearson** exam used only for **Pennsylvania** PreK-4 certification. Study materials for one do not apply to the other.    Which states require Praxis 8002 or Praxis 8006?  Confirmed data exists for seven states as of September 27, 2026 — and it shows a mutually exclusive pattern: **West Virginia, Arkansas, Virginia** — use Praxis 8002 (qualifying scores 152 / 137 / 152) as part of the 8002–8005 set; Praxis 8006 is not listed.
 **Idaho, South Dakota, Vermont** — use Praxis 8006 (qualifying score 152) for reading; 8002 is not listed.
 **South Carolina** — uses neither; reading is covered by 5205 Teaching Reading: Elementary (159). No confirmed state currently uses both. All other states: **not listed or not verified** — check your state education agency before registering. Full detail on our [state requirements tracker](https://learndiag.com/praxis-8000-series-state-requirements).    Can I take just one of them?  Yes. They are separate exams with separate registrations and scores ($79 each for a full test administration). Whether you need one or both depends entirely on your state's requirements — among confirmed states, West Virginia, Arkansas, and Virginia use Praxis 8002 while Idaho, South Dakota, and Vermont use Praxis 8006. No confirmed state currently requires both.    Which one is harder?  They test different things, so it depends on your background. Praxis 8002 rewards strong personal literacy skills. Praxis 8006 requires professional knowledge most people only get from literacy-instruction coursework — phonological awareness, structured literacy, and identifying reading difficulties. Candidates without teaching-of-reading training usually find Praxis 8006 harder.    Can I use the same study materials for both?  Only partially. General reading-comprehension practice transfers, but Praxis 8006's domains are about *how to teach* reading, which standard ELA prep does not cover. Use exam-specific materials for each test.
