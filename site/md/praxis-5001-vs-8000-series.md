# Praxis 5001 vs. the New 8000 Series: What Candidates Need to Check

*2026-08-20 · Reading time: about 8 min*

date: "2026-08-20"

If you are preparing for an elementary education license, you may now see two different sets of Praxis test codes: the familiar **Praxis 5001** series and the new **8000-series Elementary Education Fundamentals** tests. The change is real—but it does **not** mean every candidate should automatically register for the new tests.

ETS says the redesigned Elementary Education Fundamentals tests launch in **spring 2026** and are designed to replace the 5001, 7001, 5901, and 7811 series.[1] At the same time, ETS still lists Praxis 5001 as an available test product and explicitly tells candidates to make sure their certifying state accepts it.[2]

The practical takeaway: **check your current licensing requirement before choosing a test.** Your state agency, licensing organization, or educator preparation program—not a general comparison article—determines which test code and qualifying score apply to you.[7]

## Praxis 5001 vs. 8000 Series at a Glance

| Feature | Praxis 5001 series | New 8000 series |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Official name | Elementary Education: Multiple Subjects | Elementary Education Fundamentals |
| Subject test codes | 5002 RLA, 5003 Math, 5004 Social Studies, 5005 Science | 8002 RLA, 8003 Math, 8004 Social Studies, 8005 Science |
| Teaching Reading test | Not part of the 5001 combined set | 8006 Teaching Reading is part of the redesigned test family |
| Combined option | 5001 lets candidates schedule all four subject subtests in one session | No combined all-in-one option |
| Total questions by core subject tests | 245 across 5002–5005 | 299 across 8002–8005 |
| Modular category testing | Conventional subject subtests | Designed for Praxis Steps, which enables category-level testing or retesting |
| What candidates should do | Use only if currently accepted for their pathway | Use only if currently required or accepted for their pathway |

ETS describes the redesign as an update aligned with newer standards and applied classroom practice. It also highlights Science of Reading principles, Social Studies Inquiry Skills, and Science and Engineering Practices.[1]

## What Is Replacing Praxis 5001?

Praxis 5001 is the combined code for four separately timed subject tests:

- **5002:** Reading and Language Arts
- **5003:** Mathematics
- **5004:** Social Studies
- **5005:** Science

The redesigned equivalents are:

- **8002:** Reading and Language Arts
- **8003:** Mathematics
- **8004:** Social Studies
- **8005:** Science
- **8006:** Teaching Reading

The first four codes map to the same broad academic areas covered by the 5001 family, but they are not simple renumberings. ETS changed category weights and question counts in several subjects. The additional 8006 Teaching Reading test reflects a separate literacy-focused component in the new family.[1]

Just as important, **there is no new “8001” combined all-in-one option listed in the redesign.** Candidates using the new series register for the required tests separately.[1]

## How the Test Blueprints and Question Counts Change

The RLA test remains at 80 questions, but its balance shifts slightly toward reading. Math, social studies, and science all become longer.[1]

| Subject | Current test | Current questions | New test | New questions | Notable blueprint change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reading and Language Arts | 5002 | 80 | 8002 | 80 | Reading rises from about 47% to 52%; writing, speaking, and listening shifts from about 53% to 48% |
| Mathematics | 5003 | 50 | 8003 | 68 | Numbers and Operations becomes about 42%; Algebraic Thinking and Geometry, Measurement, and Data are about 29% each |
| Social Studies | 5004 | 60 | 8004 | 77 | U.S. history/government/citizenship becomes about 43%; world history and economics rises to about 27% |
| Science | 5005 | 55 | 8005 | 74 | Earth and Space Sciences is about 32%; Life Science and Physical Science are about 34% each |

These differences matter for study planning. A prep resource built only around the 5003 blueprint, for example, may not reflect the length or exact category balance of 8003. Before buying a course, practice test, or study guide, match the resource to the **exact code** you are required to take.

## What Praxis Steps Changes

The new tests are designed to work with **Praxis Steps**, a modular testing approach. ETS says Steps will allow candidates to take or retake one content category at a time, receive more targeted diagnostic feedback, and avoid repeating an entire test when only one step remains.[1]

That does not mean every 8000-series appointment is automatically broken into small categories from day one. ETS's product pages now show a concrete rollout: category-level testing through Praxis Steps launches for the **8002–8005 tests in September 2026**, with the 8006 Teaching Reading following in **2027**.[1][8] ETS's own wording also matters: the modular option applies "if your state accepts" the redesigned tests — so far, states have not announced adoption dates. Check the live registration details for your test date rather than assuming a specific Steps option is already available.

Praxis Steps also does not override licensing rules. A state or program still decides which tests count and what qualifying results candidates must earn.[7]

## Does Everyone Need to Switch From 5001?

**No.** ETS’s redesign document describes the 8000 series as replacing earlier elementary education series, but adoption is controlled by states and licensing organizations.[1][7] Transition dates may differ by jurisdiction or candidate pathway.

Meanwhile, the ETS website still lists and sells the 5001 combined test. Its current listing describes a 245-question, four-subject test lasting 4 hours and 35 minutes, with the option to take all four subtests in one session.[2] That is why it would be inaccurate to say Praxis 5001 is already universally retired or invalid.

You may still need 5001 if your current official requirement names 5001 or its subtests. You may need the 8000 series if your licensing authority has adopted the redesigned tests for your application window. If your paperwork is unclear, do not guess based on which test appears newer.

## What to Check Before You Register

Use this checklist before paying for either test family:

- **Confirm your licensing jurisdiction and certificate area.** Requirements can differ by state, license type, grade band, and pathway.
- **Find the exact required test code.** Look for 5001/5002–5005 or 8002–8006—not just “Elementary Education Praxis.”
- **Check the effective date.** A transition rule may depend on when you register, test, complete your program, or submit your license application.
- **Confirm whether individual subtests are allowed.** Do not assume the combined 5001 appointment or a particular set of 8000-series tests is required.
- **Verify the qualifying score for each test.** States and licensing organizations set their own certification and Praxis qualifying-score requirements.[7]
- **Ask how prior passing scores will be handled.** If you already passed one or more 5001 subtests, get written guidance on whether those scores remain valid during the transition.
- **Match prep materials to your code.** The new blueprints include different question counts and category weights.[1]
- **Save proof of the requirement.** Keep the official page, bulletin, or written program guidance you relied on when registering.

Start with the [ETS state requirements directory](https://praxis.ets.org/state-requirements.html), then verify the details with your state licensing agency or preparation program. ETS itself notes that each state and licensing organization establishes its own requirements.[7]

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Praxis 8001 the replacement for Praxis 5001?

ETS’s redesign identifies separate tests 8002, 8003, 8004, and 8005, plus 8006 Teaching Reading. It states that there is **no combined test option** for taking all tests in one sitting.[1] Candidates should not assume an 8001 combined test exists.

### Can I still take Praxis 5001?

ETS currently maintains a purchasable listing for Praxis 5001.[2] Availability, however, is not the same as acceptance. Confirm that your certifying organization will accept the test for your application timeline before registering.[7]

### Are the 8000-series tests just renamed versions of the 5001 subtests?

No. The subjects overlap, but ETS revised category percentages, question counts, and content emphasis. The new family also includes 8006 Teaching Reading and is designed to support Praxis Steps.[1]

### Should I use a Praxis 5001 study guide for the 8000 series?

Only as a limited content review—not as your sole blueprint. Use materials aligned to your required 8000-series code so your practice reflects the updated categories and test length.

## The Bottom Line

The Praxis 8000 series is a significant elementary education redesign, not a universal instruction for every candidate to abandon 5001 immediately. First confirm the **test code, effective date, required subjects, and qualifying scores** for your specific licensing pathway. Then build your study plan around the test you actually need.
