Q1United States History, Government and CitizenshipA fourth-grade class is completing a chart of the three branches of the federal government. Which row matches each branch with its main function?
  1. Legislative carries out federal laws, executive interprets federal laws, and judicial makes federal laws
  2. Legislative makes federal laws, executive interprets federal laws, and judicial carries out federal laws
  3. Legislative makes federal laws, executive carries out federal laws, and judicial interprets federal laws ✓
  4. Legislative interprets federal laws, executive makes federal laws, and judicial carries out federal laws

Answer: C. Article I gives Congress the power to make federal law, Article II makes the President responsible for carrying the law out, and Article III assigns interpretation of the law to the federal courts. Attaching each function to its article is the fastest way to hold the three roles apart.

  • Legislative interprets federal laws, executive makes federal laws, and judicial carries out federal laws: Rotates all three functions one branch over, sending lawmaking to the courts and law enforcement to Congress. Each function lands on the wrong article of the Constitution.
  • Legislative carries out federal laws, executive interprets federal laws, and judicial makes federal laws: Rotates the functions the opposite way, giving Congress enforcement and the courts lawmaking. The executive carries out law and Congress writes it, not the reverse.
  • Legislative makes federal laws, executive interprets federal laws, and judicial carries out federal laws: Keeps Congress in the lawmaking role but then trades execution and interpretation between the other two. The President executes federal law; the courts interpret it.
Q2United States History, Government and CitizenshipA student asks what happens when the President vetoes a bill that Congress has passed. Which statement describes the procedure the Constitution provides?
  1. Congress can override the veto with a simple majority vote in the House alone, and the bill then becomes law
  2. The veto permanently ends the bill, and Congress may not pass a similar bill again
  3. Congress can override the veto with a two-thirds vote in each chamber, and the bill then becomes law ✓
  4. The Supreme Court reviews the veto and decides whether the bill becomes law

Answer: C. Article I, Section 7 requires a two-thirds vote in the House and in the Senate before a vetoed bill becomes law. Only when both chambers reach that margin does the bill take effect without the President's signature.

  • Congress can override the veto with a simple majority vote in the House alone, and the bill then becomes law: Treats one chamber and a simple majority as enough to override. Article I, Section 7 requires two thirds in each chamber, so a single-house majority cannot revive the bill.
  • The Supreme Court reviews the veto and decides whether the bill becomes law: Moves the veto question into the judiciary, which has no role in overriding one. Judicial review asks whether a law is constitutional, not whether a veto stands.
  • The veto permanently ends the bill, and Congress may not pass a similar bill again: Reads the veto as a permanent bar on the subject matter. A veto defeats the bill as passed, but Congress may address the same subject in a new bill.
Q3United States History, Government and CitizenshipWhich statement correctly describes the Bill of Rights?
  1. It consists of the first ten amendments to the Constitution, which were added to protect individual rights ✓
  2. It is a set of rights that was added to the Constitution during the twentieth century
  3. It consists of the first seven articles of the Constitution, which created the three branches of government
  4. It is the section of the Constitution that establishes the procedure for adding amendments

Answer: A. The Bill of Rights is the first ten amendments, adopted in 1791 after critics of the new Constitution demanded explicit protections for individual liberties. The seven articles that come before the amendments are the original frame of government, not a statement of rights.

  • It consists of the first seven articles of the Constitution, which created the three branches of government: Confuses the seven original articles, which organize the branches, with the amendments that list rights. The Bill of Rights is the first ten amendments.
  • It is the section of the Constitution that establishes the procedure for adding amendments: Points to the amendment process rather than to the protections themselves. That procedure explains how amendments are made, not which rights are guaranteed.
  • It is a set of rights that was added to the Constitution during the twentieth century: Places the Bill of Rights in the wrong century. It was adopted in 1791, two years after the Constitution took effect.
Q4United States History, Government and CitizenshipA civics class is listing the protections named in the First Amendment. Which list is correct?
  1. Protection against unreasonable searches and seizures and the privilege against self-incrimination
  2. The right to keep and bear arms and the right not to be forced to quarter soldiers
  3. Freedom of religion, speech, the press, assembly, and petition ✓
  4. The right to a speedy and public trial by an impartial jury

Answer: C. The First Amendment protects five freedoms: religion, speech, the press, assembly, and the right to petition the government. Each of the other lists names protections that appear in later amendments to the Constitution.

  • The right to keep and bear arms and the right not to be forced to quarter soldiers: Names protections from the Second and Third Amendments. Bearing arms and quartering soldiers are addressed two amendments later, not in the First.
  • Protection against unreasonable searches and seizures and the privilege against self-incrimination: Names protections from the Fourth and Fifth Amendments. Searches, seizures, and self-incrimination are addressed after the First Amendment.
  • The right to a speedy and public trial by an impartial jury: Names a protection from the Sixth Amendment. Jury trial rights are a criminal-procedure guarantee, not one of the five freedoms the First Amendment lists.
Q5United States History, Government and CitizenshipWhich sequence describes the most commonly used process for proposing and ratifying a constitutional amendment?
  1. An amendment is proposed by a simple majority in Congress and ratified by a majority of the states
  2. An amendment is proposed by the President and ratified by a two-thirds vote of the Supreme Court
  3. An amendment is proposed by the state governors and ratified by the House of Representatives
  4. An amendment is proposed by a two-thirds vote in each chamber of Congress and ratified by three-fourths of the states ✓

Answer: D. The most commonly used process for amending the U.S. Constitution requires two-thirds of both houses of Congress to propose an amendment and three-fourths of the states to ratify it.

  • An amendment is proposed by the President and ratified by a two-thirds vote of the Supreme Court: Gives the proposing role to the President and the ratifying role to the courts. Neither can propose or ratify an amendment; that authority belongs to Congress and the states.
  • An amendment is proposed by a simple majority in Congress and ratified by a majority of the states: Uses ordinary-legislation margins for a constitutional amendment. Simple majorities pass a law, but Article V requires two thirds to propose and three fourths to ratify.
  • An amendment is proposed by the state governors and ratified by the House of Representatives: Moves both steps to bodies that do not hold them. Governors do not propose amendments and the House does not ratify them; Congress proposes and the states ratify.
Q6United States History, Government and CitizenshipA state sets the minimum age at which residents may obtain a driver's license within its borders. Which constitutional principle best explains the state's authority to do this?
  1. Reserved powers, because powers the Constitution does not give to the federal government remain with the states ✓
  2. The supremacy clause, because federal law takes priority over conflicting state law
  3. Judicial review, because courts decide which level of government may act
  4. The full faith and credit clause, because each state must honor the official acts of the other states

Answer: A. The Tenth Amendment reserves to the states the powers the Constitution neither delegates to the federal government nor forbids to the states, and licensing drivers is a standard example. Setting a minimum licensing age is a state function that no enumerated federal power covers.

  • The supremacy clause, because federal law takes priority over conflicting state law: Applies a rule that settles conflicts between federal and state law rather than one that assigns an unused power. The supremacy clause says nothing about who sets licensing rules.
  • Judicial review, because courts decide which level of government may act: Substitutes the judiciary's reviewing role for the rule that allocates powers. Judicial review settles whether an action is constitutional; it is not the source of state authority.
  • The full faith and credit clause, because each state must honor the official acts of the other states: Cites a clause about honoring other states' official acts. Full faith and credit governs how states treat one another's records, not which level of government licenses drivers.
Q7United States History, Government and CitizenshipDelegates at the Constitutional Convention disagreed about how the states should be represented in Congress. How did the Great Compromise resolve that disagreement?
  1. It created a two-chamber Congress in which both chambers apportioned seats strictly by state population
  2. It created a two-chamber Congress with representation by population in the House and equal representation for each state in the Senate ✓
  3. It created a single-chamber Congress in which each state had exactly one vote
  4. It left the question of representation for the Supreme Court to settle in a later case

Answer: B. The Great Compromise of 1787 combined the population-based representation favored by the larger states with the equal state representation favored by the smaller ones. That two-chamber structure is why the House and the Senate count their members so differently today.

  • It created a single-chamber Congress in which each state had exactly one vote: Keeps the equal-state vote but drops the second chamber. The compromise added a population-based House rather than settling on a single chamber.
  • It created a two-chamber Congress in which both chambers apportioned seats strictly by state population: Keeps the population rule but applies it to both chambers. Equal representation for each state in the Senate is the half of the compromise this option removes.
  • It left the question of representation for the Supreme Court to settle in a later case: Sends a question the convention settled itself to the courts. Delegates agreed on the two-chamber structure in 1787 and the Supreme Court played no part in it.
Q8United States History, Government and CitizenshipWhich Supreme Court case established the principle of judicial review, under which courts may decline to apply a law they find inconsistent with the Constitution?
  1. McCulloch v. Maryland (1819)
  2. Gibbons v. Ogden (1824)
  3. Miranda v. Arizona (1966)
  4. Marbury v. Madison (1803) ✓

Answer: D. Marbury v. Madison established judicial review when the Court declined to apply a section of a federal statute it found inconsistent with the Constitution. McCulloch concerned implied federal powers, Gibbons concerned federal authority over interstate commerce, and Miranda concerned criminal procedure.

  • McCulloch v. Maryland (1819): Names the case that upheld implied federal powers, including the authority to charter a national bank. McCulloch expanded federal power; it did not announce judicial review.
  • Gibbons v. Ogden (1824): Names the case that defined federal authority over interstate commerce. Gibbons settled a steamboat monopoly dispute rather than the courts' power to review statutes.
  • Miranda v. Arizona (1966): Names a criminal-procedure case about warnings required before custodial questioning. Miranda regulated police interrogation long after judicial review was established.
Q9United States History, Government and CitizenshipThe Declaration of Independence states that governments derive their just powers from which source?
  1. The wealth of the nation
  2. The rulings of the courts
  3. The authority of a hereditary monarch
  4. The consent of the governed ✓

Answer: D. The Declaration of Independence grounds legitimate government in the consent of the governed and argues that people may alter a government that becomes destructive of the rights it was formed to protect. That claim about the source of authority is the document's central argument.

  • The authority of a hereditary monarch: Reverses the Declaration's argument by locating authority in inherited rank. The document was written to justify separating from a monarchy, not to defend one.
  • The rulings of the courts: Places the origin of authority in the judiciary. The Declaration treats the people as the source of government power rather than courts deciding what is lawful.
  • The wealth of the nation: Confuses resources with legitimacy. The Declaration rests authority on consent, not on how much property or wealth a country holds.
Q10United States History, Government and CitizenshipWhich statement describes what the Twenty-Sixth Amendment to the Constitution did?
  1. It gave 18-year-olds the right to hold federal office
  2. It set 21 as the minimum voting age in federal elections
  3. It set 18 as the minimum voting age in federal elections only
  4. It set 18 as the minimum voting age in federal, state, and local elections ✓

Answer: D. The Twenty-Sixth Amendment, ratified in 1971, lowered the minimum voting age to 18 and covers state and local elections as well as federal ones. Establishing a single national age for every election was the point of the amendment.

  • It set 21 as the minimum voting age in federal elections: Moves the age in the wrong direction and limits it to federal elections. Twenty-one was the age many states required before 1971, and the amendment lowered rather than raised it.
  • It gave 18-year-olds the right to hold federal office: Confuses voting with holding office. The amendment addresses who may vote; qualifications for federal office are set elsewhere in the Constitution.
  • It set 18 as the minimum voting age in federal elections only: Splits federal elections from state and local ones, which the amendment deliberately does not do. Covering every election was the reason for its passage.
Q11United States History, Government and CitizenshipA class is sorting examples of civic participation. Which pair correctly distinguishes a legal duty from a voluntary responsibility of citizens?
  1. Obeying traffic laws is a voluntary responsibility, while voting in every election is a legal duty.
  2. Voting in a presidential election is a legal duty, while paying income taxes is a voluntary responsibility
  3. Running for local office is a legal duty, while registering to vote is a voluntary responsibility
  4. Serving on a jury when summoned is a legal duty, while volunteering with a community organization is a voluntary responsibility ✓

Answer: D. Legal duties are the things the law requires, such as jury service when summoned, paying taxes, and obeying the law. Voluntary responsibilities are the things citizens choose to do, such as voting and community service.

  • Voting in a presidential election is a legal duty, while paying income taxes is a voluntary responsibility: Reverses both labels. Voting is a civic choice, while paying income taxes is legally required, and failing to file and pay carries legal consequences.
  • Running for local office is a legal duty, while registering to vote is a voluntary responsibility: Treats holding office as required and registration as optional. Running for office is a personal choice, and registration is a legal prerequisite for voting in nearly every state.
  • Obeying traffic laws is a voluntary responsibility, while voting in every election is a legal duty.: Obeying traffic laws is a legal duty, not a voluntary responsibility. Voting is an important civic responsibility, but it is not legally required in every election.
Q12United States History, Government and CitizenshipIn 1803 the United States purchased a large territory from France that greatly expanded the nation's land area. What is this acquisition called?
  1. The Mexican Cession
  2. The Gadsden Purchase
  3. The Alaska Purchase
  4. The Louisiana Purchase ✓

Answer: D. The Louisiana Purchase transferred a vast area west of the Mississippi River from France to the United States in 1803. The Gadsden Purchase and the Alaska Purchase came later in the nineteenth century, and the Mexican Cession followed the war with Mexico.

  • The Gadsden Purchase: Names an 1853 purchase of land from Mexico along the present southern border of Arizona and New Mexico. It came fifty years later and covered a far smaller area.
  • The Alaska Purchase: Names the 1867 purchase of Alaska from Russia. It came more than sixty years later and involved territory in the far northwest rather than the Mississippi valley.
  • The Mexican Cession: Names land transferred by Mexico after the war of 1846 to 1848. That territory came from Mexico in a separate transaction decades later.
Q13United States History, Government and CitizenshipWhich TWO of the following are examples of one branch of government limiting the power of another branch?
  1. The Senate must confirm the President's appointments of federal judges ✓
  2. The Supreme Court may decline to apply a federal law it finds inconsistent with the Constitution ✓
  3. Each chamber of Congress sets its own rules of procedure
  4. Congress reviews a bill before voting on it.
  5. The President appoints the heads of the executive departments

Answers: A and B. A check requires one branch to act before another can succeed. Senate confirmation gives the legislative branch a say over the President's judicial appointments, and judicial review gives the courts a say over laws Congress has passed. The other three items are ordinary functions a single branch performs on its own.

  • The President appoints the heads of the executive departments: Describes the executive branch staffing itself, not constraining another branch. A check requires a second branch whose consent or refusal decides the outcome.
  • Each chamber of Congress sets its own rules of procedure: Mistakes internal housekeeping for a check on another branch. Setting its own rules is a power each chamber exercises over itself, not a lever over the President or the courts.
  • Congress reviews a bill before voting on it.: Reviewing a bill before voting is an internal legislative procedure. It does not give Congress a direct check on another branch of government.
Q14Geography, Anthropology and SociologyWhich line on a globe marks zero degrees latitude and divides Earth into the Northern and Southern Hemispheres?
  1. The equator ✓
  2. The Tropic of Cancer
  3. The prime meridian
  4. The International Date Line

Answer: A. Latitude is measured in degrees north or south of the equator, which makes the equator the zero-degree line and the boundary between the Northern and Southern Hemispheres. The other lines named mark longitude or a latitude other than zero.

  • The prime meridian: Confuses a longitude line with the latitude baseline. The prime meridian marks zero degrees longitude and separates Earth into eastern and western halves.
  • The Tropic of Cancer: Names a line of latitude other than zero. The Tropic of Cancer lies near twenty-three and a half degrees north, well away from the equator.
  • The International Date Line: Names a longitude line that marks where the calendar date changes. It runs near the one hundred eightieth meridian and has nothing to do with the equator.
Q15Geography, Anthropology and SociologyA class investigates how building a dam changes a river's flow and the farming in the valley downstream. Which of the five themes of geography does this topic address most directly?
  1. Human-environment interaction ✓
  2. Location
  3. Region
  4. Movement

Answer: A. The five themes are location, place, human-environment interaction, movement, and region. Building a dam is a human modification of the environment, and the changed farming downstream is an effect of that modification, which is what human-environment interaction describes.

  • Location: Focuses on where something is rather than on how people change it. Absolute or relative position is the theme of location; a dam altering a river is a modification of the environment.
  • Region: Answers a question about shared characteristics instead of about change. Region groups places by common features, while the dam and the altered farming concern human impact on a place.
  • Movement: Points to the flow of people, goods, or ideas rather than environmental change. Movement covers things shifting across space, not the reshaping of a river and its farmland.
Q16Geography, Anthropology and SociologyWhich term describes a narrow strip of land that joins two larger land areas and has water on both sides?
  1. Strait
  2. Isthmus ✓
  3. Peninsula
  4. Delta

Answer: B. An isthmus is the land bridge itself, such as the strip joining North America and South America. A peninsula is land bordered by water on three sides, a strait is a narrow water passage, and a delta is sediment deposited where a river meets a larger body of water.

  • Peninsula: Describes land bordered by water on three sides rather than a land bridge. A peninsula reaches out into water instead of connecting two larger land areas.
  • Strait: Names a water passage instead of a landform. A strait is a narrow channel of water between two bodies of land, which is nearly the opposite of the feature described.
  • Delta: Names sediment deposited at a river mouth. A delta forms where a river enters a larger body of water and is not a connecting strip of land.
Q17Geography, Anthropology and SociologyA family moves to another country because of armed conflict at home and better-paying work available in the destination country. Which statement labels these two causes correctly?
  1. Both the conflict and the job opportunities are pull factors
  2. Both the conflict and the job opportunities are push factors
  3. The conflict is a pull factor, and the job opportunities are a push factor
  4. The conflict is a push factor, and the job opportunities are a pull factor ✓

Answer: D. Push factors are conditions that drive people away from a place, and pull factors are conditions that attract them to a new one. Conflict pushes people out of the origin country, while job opportunities pull them toward the destination.

  • The conflict is a pull factor, and the job opportunities are a push factor: Swaps the two labels. Conflict drives people away from a place, which makes it a push factor, while job opportunities draw them in, which makes them a pull factor.
  • Both the conflict and the job opportunities are push factors: Treats a destination's attractions as something that repels. Job opportunities draw the family toward the new country, so they cannot be a reason for leaving.
  • Both the conflict and the job opportunities are pull factors: Treats a condition in the origin country as an attraction. Armed conflict drives people out and cannot function as something pulling them toward a destination.
Q18Geography, Anthropology and SociologyTwo countries have the same total population, but one covers a much larger land area than the other. Which statement about their population density is correct?
  1. The two countries cannot be compared without knowing each one's birth rate
  2. The two countries have the same population density because their populations are equal
  3. The country with the larger land area has the higher population density
  4. The country with the smaller land area has the higher population density ✓

Answer: D. Population density is the number of people per unit of area, so dividing an equal population by a smaller area gives the larger result. Equal populations alone say nothing about density until the area is taken into account.

  • The country with the larger land area has the higher population density: Divides the same population by the larger area, which lowers density instead of raising it. Spreading an equal number of people over more land leaves fewer per unit of area.
  • The two countries have the same population density because their populations are equal: Treats population as the only input and ignores area. Density is a ratio of people to land, so equal populations give equal densities only when the areas are equal too.
  • The two countries cannot be compared without knowing each one's birth rate: Adds a variable the ratio does not need. Density compares people with area, while birth rates affect how a population changes over time, not how densely it is settled now.
Q19Geography, Anthropology and SociologyWhich term describes the practice of judging another culture by the standards of one's own culture?
  1. Acculturation
  2. Cultural diffusion
  3. Ethnocentrism ✓
  4. Cultural relativism

Answer: C. Ethnocentrism is the habit of measuring other cultures against one's own and treating the familiar as normal. Cultural relativism is the contrasting approach of trying to understand a practice within its own cultural context.

  • Cultural relativism: Names the opposite approach. Cultural relativism tries to understand a practice inside its own context instead of measuring it against an outside standard.
  • Acculturation: Names a process of cultural change rather than a way of judging. Acculturation describes what happens when groups adopt elements of one another's cultures over time.
  • Cultural diffusion: Names the spread of cultural traits rather than an evaluative stance. Diffusion explains how practices move between societies, not how an observer judges one.
Q20Geography, Anthropology and SociologyIn sociology, which statement best describes what the term social institution means?
  1. A stable pattern of roles and rules organized around a basic social need, such as education or family life ✓
  2. A law passed by a national legislature and enforced by the police
  3. A short-lived group formed to carry out a single task and then disband
  4. A building where members of a community gather for public meetings

Answer: A. Sociologists use institution for the enduring arrangements that meet a society's basic needs, such as family, education, and government. The term names the pattern of roles and rules rather than the physical building that houses it.

  • A building where members of a community gather for public meetings: Uses the everyday sense of the word for a physical place. A building is a setting for activity; the sociological institution is the enduring pattern of roles and rules.
  • A short-lived group formed to carry out a single task and then disband: Describes a temporary arrangement instead of an enduring one. Institutions persist across generations, while a task group ends when its task is finished.
  • A law passed by a national legislature and enforced by the police: Points to one enacted rule rather than a durable pattern. A law can be part of an institution, but the institution also includes roles and practices that outlast any statute.
Q21Geography, Anthropology and SociologyWhich example best illustrates what sociologists call a primary group?
  1. A lecture class of two hundred students taught by one instructor
  2. A committee that meets twice and then disbands after filing its report
  3. A family whose members know one another well and interact face to face over many years ✓
  4. A professional association with tens of thousands of members nationwide

Answer: C. A primary group is small, personal, and lasting, and members value the relationships in it for their own sake. Family and close friendship groups are the standard examples, in contrast with the large, impersonal, and often temporary ties of secondary groups.

  • A lecture class of two hundred students taught by one instructor: Describes a large, impersonal setting typical of a secondary group. Members here do not know one another personally and the shared task matters more than the ties.
  • A professional association with tens of thousands of members nationwide: Describes a large-scale formal organization rather than a small personal group. Associations of that size are impersonal, which is the opposite of the primary-group pattern.
  • A committee that meets twice and then disbands after filing its report: Selects a group defined by a task and a short life. Primary relationships endure and are valued for themselves rather than for finishing a job and ending.
Q22Geography, Anthropology and SociologyA teacher is checking a review sheet about the grid of latitude and longitude. Which TWO of the following statements are accurate?
  1. Lines of latitude run east-west and measure distance north or south of the equator ✓
  2. Lines of longitude run north-south and measure distance east or west of the prime meridian ✓
  3. Lines of longitude are parallel to one another and never meet
  4. Lines of latitude measure distance east or west of the prime meridian
  5. The prime meridian is the line of zero degrees latitude

Answers: A and B. Latitude and longitude form a grid of two different measurements. Lines of latitude circle Earth east-west and give a position north or south of the equator, while lines of longitude run from pole to pole and give a position east or west of the prime meridian.

  • Lines of latitude measure distance east or west of the prime meridian: Assigns the east-west measurement to latitude, which is the other coordinate. Latitude measures north or south of the equator; east-west position is measured by longitude.
  • The prime meridian is the line of zero degrees latitude: Gives the prime meridian a latitude value. The prime meridian is the zero-degree line of longitude, while the equator is the line of zero degrees latitude.
  • Lines of longitude are parallel to one another and never meet: Assumes meridians behave like lines of latitude. Lines of longitude converge at the poles and are not parallel, while lines of latitude stay parallel to one another.
Q23World History and EconomicsA history textbook labels dates on a timeline with the abbreviations B.C.E. and C.E. Which statement explains what these labels mean?
  1. C.E. labels dates after the traditional starting point of the common era, and B.C.E. labels dates before it ✓
  2. C.E. labels events recorded in Europe, and B.C.E. labels events recorded everywhere else
  3. B.C.E. and C.E. name two separate calendar systems that are never used on the same timeline
  4. B.C.E. labels dates after the traditional starting point of the common era, and C.E. labels dates before it

Answer: A. B.C.E. and C.E. divide a timeline at a single conventional point. Dates labeled B.C.E. count backward from that point and dates labeled C.E. count forward from it, so the two labels work as halves of one continuous scale.

  • B.C.E. labels dates after the traditional starting point of the common era, and C.E. labels dates before it: Reverses the direction of the two labels. Dates before the dividing point carry B.C.E. and dates after it carry C.E., not the other way around.
  • B.C.E. and C.E. name two separate calendar systems that are never used on the same timeline: Treats two halves of one scale as competing calendars. B.C.E. and C.E. appear together on the same timeline and meet at a single dividing point.
  • C.E. labels events recorded in Europe, and B.C.E. labels events recorded everywhere else: Ties the labels to where a record was made rather than to when an event happened. Both labels are used for events anywhere in the world.
Q24World History and EconomicsWhich pairing of an early civilization with the river system it grew up along is correct?
  1. Mesopotamia developed along the Tigris and Euphrates rivers ✓
  2. Mesopotamia developed along the Nile River
  3. Ancient Egypt developed along the Indus River
  4. Ancient Egypt developed along the Tigris and Euphrates rivers

Answer: A. Mesopotamia, whose name refers to land between rivers, grew up in the valleys of the Tigris and the Euphrates. Egypt developed along the Nile and the Indus Valley civilization developed along the Indus, so each of the other pairings attaches a civilization to the wrong river.

  • Ancient Egypt developed along the Tigris and Euphrates rivers: Attaches the Tigris and Euphrates valley to Egypt. Egypt developed along the Nile, which lies in northeastern Africa and floods on a different cycle.
  • Mesopotamia developed along the Nile River: Attaches the Nile to Mesopotamia. The Nile valley was the setting for Egyptian civilization, while Mesopotamia lay between the Tigris and the Euphrates.
  • Ancient Egypt developed along the Indus River: Attaches the Indus to Egypt. The Indus River supported the Indus Valley civilization in South Asia rather than Egyptian civilization in northeastern Africa.
Q25World History and EconomicsWhich ancient Greek city-state is the standard example of a direct democracy, in which eligible citizens voted on laws in an assembly rather than electing representatives to decide for them?
  1. Athens ✓
  2. Corinth
  3. Sparta
  4. Thebes

Answer: A. Athens is the classical example of direct democracy: eligible citizens, a group limited to adult males of citizen status, voted on laws and policy in the assembly in person. The other city-states named are better known for other forms of government or for their military roles.

  • Sparta: Names a city-state known for a rigid military order and a mixed constitution rather than for citizen lawmaking. Sparta is remembered for its army, not for an assembly deciding laws.
  • Thebes: Names a city-state prominent in Greek politics and warfare rather than in the development of direct democracy. Thebes is remembered for its military role, not for pioneering citizen assemblies.
  • Corinth: Names a commercial city-state rather than the classical democracy. Corinth was a trading power, and its government is not the standard example of citizens voting directly on laws.
Q26World History and EconomicsIn which country did the Industrial Revolution that began in the late eighteenth century C.E. first take hold?
  1. Great Britain ✓
  2. France
  3. Germany
  4. The United States

Answer: A. The Industrial Revolution first took hold in Great Britain, which had coal, iron, capital, and a large home market for manufactured goods. Mechanized textile production and the railway boom then spread from Britain to the European continent and to North America.

  • France: Names a country that industrialized after Britain and adopted mechanized textile production more slowly. France had a large economy but reached factory production later and on a smaller scale.
  • The United States: Names a country where industrialization arrived after Britain's takeoff. American factory development drew heavily on technology and capital that originated in Britain.
  • Germany: Names a region that industrialized later, after the first British factories and the spread of railways. The German states reached large-scale factory production well into the nineteenth century.
Q27World History and EconomicsA school can afford either new science equipment or new library books, but not both. What is the economic term for the value of the option that is given up?
  1. Opportunity cost ✓
  2. Profit
  3. Inflation
  4. Supply

Answer: A. Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative that a choice gives up. When the school buys the science equipment, the forgone books are the opportunity cost of that decision, and the concept exists precisely because resources are limited.

  • Inflation: Names a change in the general price level rather than the value of a forgone choice. Inflation describes rising prices across an economy, not what a decision costs in lost alternatives.
  • Profit: Names a return to a business rather than the value of what was given up. Profit is the revenue a seller keeps after costs, which is a different idea from the alternative a chooser forgoes.
  • Supply: Names the amount offered for sale instead of the cost of choosing. Supply describes what producers bring to market at various prices, not what a decision makes you give up.
Q28World History and EconomicsIn a market, all other things being equal, what happens to the quantity demanded of a good when its price rises?
  1. The quantity demanded stays the same
  2. The quantity demanded decreases ✓
  3. The quantity demanded increases
  4. The quantity demanded falls to zero

Answer: B. The law of demand holds that quantity demanded moves in the opposite direction from price when other influences are held constant. A higher price makes the good less attractive relative to alternatives and to the buyer's budget, so buyers purchase less of it.

  • The quantity demanded increases: Has the two variables moving in the same direction. If buyers wanted more of a good as its price rose, the relationship between price and quantity demanded would be the reverse of what it is.
  • The quantity demanded stays the same: Assumes buyers ignore a price change altogether. The law of demand describes a systematic decrease in quantity demanded rather than no change at all.
  • The quantity demanded falls to zero: Treats an ordinary price increase as ending all purchases. A higher price reduces the quantity demanded; it does not normally wipe out demand for the good.
Q29World History and EconomicsIn economics, which statement describes inflation?
  1. A decline in the number of workers available to employers
  2. An increase in the total quantity of goods and services an economy produces
  3. A general rise in the level of prices across an economy over time ✓
  4. A rise in the price of a single good while other prices stay unchanged

Answer: C. Inflation is a sustained increase in the general price level, measured across a broad basket of goods and services rather than at a single store. A one-time increase in the price of one product is a relative price change, not inflation.

  • A rise in the price of a single good while other prices stay unchanged: Limits the change to one product and calls it inflation. A single price rising while others hold steady is a relative price change, not a change in the general price level.
  • An increase in the total quantity of goods and services an economy produces: Describes growth in output instead of a change in prices. Producing more than before is economic growth, which can occur while prices remain stable.
  • A decline in the number of workers available to employers: Describes a change in the labor force rather than in prices. Fewer available workers can affect wages and output, but inflation is specifically about the general price level.
Q30World History and EconomicsA class is building a diagram of the factors of production. Which TWO of the following are factors of production as economists use the term?
  1. The price buyers agree to pay for a finished good
  2. Labor, meaning the human effort and time devoted to production ✓
  3. Land, meaning the natural resources used in production ✓
  4. The number of units a firm sells in a year
  5. Money held in a checking account

Answers: B and C. Land and labor are two of the four factors of production. Land includes natural resources used to produce goods and services, and labor is the physical or mental work people provide. Capital means produced resources such as tools, machines, and buildings used to produce goods and services, while entrepreneurship involves organizing production and taking business risks. Money held in a checking account is not a factor of production in this classification: money can be used to buy capital resources, but it is not itself a production resource.

  • Money held in a checking account: Treats a medium of exchange as an input. Money buys the tools and machines that count as capital, but a cash balance is not itself one of the resources that produce output.
  • The price buyers agree to pay for a finished good: Reads an outcome of exchange as an input to production. Price is settled after goods exist and is not a resource that firms combine in order to produce them.
  • The number of units a firm sells in a year: Confuses a measure of what was sold with a resource used to make things. Units sold report results rather than one of the inputs production requires.
