Praxis 8004 Social Studies: What the Test Covers and How to Prepare

2026-09-15 · Last verified 2026-09-15 · Reading time: about 11 min
Test requirements vary by state, preparation program, and effective date. Confirm your required test code with your state licensing agency or educator preparation program before registering.

Praxis 8004 — officially Elementary Education Fundamentals: Social Studies — is the social studies content test in ETS’s redesigned Elementary Education Fundamentals family. It is a 100-minute, 77-question selected-response test that first administers on 2026-03-09[1][5]. It covers United States history, government and citizenship, geography, anthropology and sociology, and world history and economics: teacher content knowledge, not a social studies methods exam.

Social studies is the subject where a wrong fact does the most damage — to your score and to a child’s understanding. So this page is deliberately narrow about what it will assert. Every claim below traces to a numbered official source, and the items we could not verify are named instead of guessed.

This page explains what the test covers, who it is for, how it is structured, and how to confirm whether it is the test you should register for. It also includes a free 30-question practice mini test in which every wrong option is explained, not only the correct one.

Is Praxis 8004 the Right Test for You?

This is the most important question on this page, and it is not one we can answer for you.

Praxis 8004 is a social studies content test. Candidates who need it are typically in one of these situations:

  • Their state requires a social studies content exam for elementary licensure and has adopted 8004 for that purpose.
  • Their educator preparation program requires a social studies content exam as a program completion or exit requirement.
  • They already hold an elementary license and are adding a social studies subject-area endorsement that names a Praxis test.

What we can verify, and what we cannot. Two states are confirmed in our fact library. In both, 8004 is the test used, with a state-set qualifying score:

State8004 statusQualifying score
West VirginiaListed on the state’s ETS requirements page[2]147[2]
ArkansasListed on the state’s ETS requirements page[3]130[3]

For the remaining 48 states, adoption status is not verified. We do not publish adoption claims we have not confirmed against a state agency or an ETS state requirements page, because a wrong answer here costs you a test fee and a testing window.

One timing detail is worth knowing if you are testing in Arkansas: that state’s page states that 5000-series tests are accepted through 2027-08-31, and that 5000-series and 8000-series scores may be combined during that period, provided each of the four content areas is satisfied by at least one passing test[3]. Read that page yourself rather than relying on our summary.

How to confirm your test code in about five minutes:

  1. Open your state’s ETS requirements page: https://praxis.ets.org/state-requirements/<state>-tests.html. (State names are written as one word, no hyphen — westvirginia, not west-virginia.)
  2. Search the page for Social Studies and check whether it lists 8004, 5004, or both. Note any qualifying score shown.
  3. If the page is ambiguous or lists both an old and a new code, contact your educator preparation program or state licensing agency directly and ask which code applies to your certification pathway.

If you are unsure after both steps, do not register yet. The fee is $79.00 and retake windows are limited[1].

What Praxis 8004 Actually Tests

The 77 questions are distributed across three official content categories. Unlike the science test in the same family, the 8004 categories are published item counts rather than third-party estimates, which means the weights below are safe to plan against[5].

United States History, Government and Citizenship — 33 questions (about 43%)

The largest category. Expect items on the founding documents and the structure of government, the three branches and the relationships among them, citizenship and civic participation, and major eras of United States history.

Typical question shapes:

  • Match a branch of government, or a level of government, to the function it performs.
  • Identify the constitutional principle that explains why an institution can act as it does in a short scenario.
  • Read a brief description of a historical development and identify the era, cause, or consequence it belongs to.
  • Distinguish a legal duty from a voluntary responsibility of citizenship.

Geography, Anthropology and Sociology — 23 questions (about 30%)

Maps and spatial reasoning, the themes of geography, human-environment relationships, population and migration, and the introductory concepts of anthropology and sociology — culture, norms, groups, and institutions.

Typical question shapes:

  • Interpret a map, a coordinate, or a landform description and name what it shows.
  • Classify a real-world situation under the geographic theme or process it illustrates.
  • Match an anthropological or sociological term to its definition or to a short example.
  • Explain a demographic pattern such as density or migration in terms of its inputs.

World History and Economics — 21 questions (about 27%)

Early and classical civilizations, world-historical turning points, historical chronology and periodization, and the foundational economics concepts that elementary curricula teach: scarcity, choice, opportunity cost, supply and demand, inflation, and the factors of production.

Typical question shapes:

  • Place events or civilizations correctly on a timeline labeled in B.C.E. and C.E.
  • Match a civilization, turning point, or invention to the place or period it belongs to.
  • Apply an economics concept to a short everyday decision and name the concept.
  • Predict the direction of a basic market relationship from a stated change.

Teaching scenario items (approximately 10–15%)

ETS notes that the Elementary Education Fundamentals tests include 10–15% Tasks of Teaching items[2][3]. These embed content in a classroom situation and ask what you would do as the teacher. They are distributed across the three categories above, not a separate section.

A note on what we are not telling you. ETS publishes the category names and item counts above. It does not publish a public sub-skill weighting for 8004. The “typical question shapes” are our professional reading of the category definitions, not an ETS item list. Treat them as orientation, not as a specification.

How We Write and Explain Every Practice Item

Most practice banks tell you whether you were right. That is not enough in social studies, because the reason an item is interesting is almost always the wrong option you were drawn to. Distractors in this subject are usually adjacent facts — the right answer to a slightly different question — and until you can say why the adjacent fact is the wrong answer here, you have not learned the content.

So every item in our 8004 bank carries an explanation for the correct option and a separate explanation for each of the other options, written to name the specific misconception that option represents rather than to say that it is incorrect. Here is the format, using a real item from the bank:

Part of the itemWhat the learner sees
StemA class investigates how building a dam changes a river’s flow and the farming in the valley downstream. Which of the five themes of geography does this topic address most directly?
Correct optionHuman-environment interaction
Why it is correctThe five themes are location, place, human-environment interaction, movement, and region. Building a dam is a human modification of the environment, and the changed farming downstream is an effect of that modification.
Why “Movement” is wrongPoints to the flow of people, goods, or ideas rather than environmental change. Movement covers things shifting across space, not the reshaping of a river and its farmland.
Why “Region” is wrongAnswers a question about shared characteristics instead of about change. Region groups places by common features, while the dam and the altered farming concern human impact on a place.
Why “Location” is wrongFocuses on where something is rather than on how people change it. Absolute or relative position is the theme of location; a dam altering a river is a modification of the environment.

Three further rules govern the bank, and they exist because social studies is where a prep site is most likely to be quietly wrong:

  • B.C.E. and C.E., matching the official materials. ETS uses this notation for 8004[4], and our items and explanations use it too, so the practice looks like the test.
  • No question that requires taking a side. Items avoid partisan positions, religious doctrine, contested territorial claims, and group characterizations. Where a topic has a standard textbook treatment, the item uses that treatment and nothing more; where it does not, we do not write the item.
  • No third-party summaries as sources. Content is written to the official category definitions and to standard reference facts. A study-guide site’s paraphrase of a topic is a lead, never a source of record — several of the most widely repeated 8000-series “facts” circulating online are simply wrong, and we list two of them under Scoring below.

We also do not hide the fact that the bank is new. The 30 items behind this page are machine-drafted and have not yet been through human review, and each one is flagged as review_status: draft until it is. Anything on this page we could not verify against an official source is named in the notes at the bottom rather than smoothed over.

How Praxis 8004 Differs from Praxis 5004

Candidates preparing for the older social studies test will find the content familiar. What changed is the structure of the test family, the length, and the category weights:

What it isSocial Studies subtest within Elementary Education: Multiple SubjectsStandalone test in the Elementary Education Fundamentals family
Questions60[7]77[5]
Time60 minutes[7]100 minutes[5]
Content categories27 / 18 / 15 (45% / 30% / 25%)[7]33 / 23 / 21 (about 43% / 30% / 27%)[5]
Dating convention in materialsNot specified on the test pageB.C.E. / C.E.[4]
CalculatorNot providedNot provided[1]
Modular category retakesNot availablePraxis Steps lists 8401 / 8402 / 8403 for 8004[1]
Legacy statusRetires with the 5000 series in 2028-08[2][3]Current test in the 8000 series[5]

Three practical consequences:

  1. More time, more items, one subject. 5004 gave you a minute per question inside a longer combined test. 8004 gives you about 78 seconds per question on a single subject, which is comfortable if you know the content and unforgiving if you are guessing.
  2. The weights are close to 5004 but not identical. United States history, government and citizenship remains the largest category in both. The 8004 split is published by ETS for this test, so plan against it directly rather than converting from a 5004 guide[5].
  3. Do not assume the codes are interchangeable. They are different tests with different registrations and different score reporting. If your state still lists 5004, prepare for 5004; if it has moved to the 8000 series, prepare for 8004[2][3].

If you are working from the older guide for the previous series, start here: Praxis 5004 Social Studies study guide — its timelines and review tables cover the same content area, and its own page states plainly that it does not cover 8004.

Scoring: What You Can and Cannot Predict

Praxis 8004 is scored on a 100–200 scale[6]. Your qualifying score is set by your state licensing agency or preparation program, not by ETS and not nationally[2][3]. The two 8004 values we have confirmed are West Virginia’s 147 and Arkansas’s 130[2][3]; both sit inside the range of confirmed 8000-series qualifying scores, which runs from 126 to 152 across the family.

Two claims circulate online that are wrong, and that we do not repeat:

  • That the 8000 series uses a 100–300 scale with a 240 passing score. It does not. The scale is 100–200, and the verified qualifying scores in the 8000 series sit between 126 and 152[2][3][6].
  • That there is a single national cut score for 8004. There is not. Any page that gives you one number without naming a state is guessing[2][3].

Because there is no public official conversion table from raw items to scaled score for Praxis 8004, we do not offer a scaled score calculator for this test. A practice percentage is a practice percentage. Presenting it as a predicted scaled score would be a fabrication, and it is the kind of number people make registration decisions on.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Praxis 8004?

Praxis 8004 is Elementary Education Fundamentals: Social Studies, a 100-minute, 77-question selected-response test on social studies content for elementary teachers. It is part of the 8000 series and first administers on 2026-03-09[1][5].

What does Praxis 8004 cover?

Three content categories: United States History, Government and Citizenship (33 questions), Geography, Anthropology and Sociology (23 questions), and World History and Economics (21 questions)[5].

Is Praxis 8004 the same as Praxis 5004?

No. 5004 is the Social Studies subtest of the Elementary Education: Multiple Subjects test (5001), listed by ETS at 60 questions in 60 minutes. 8004 is a standalone test in the 8000 series at 77 questions in 100 minutes. Both cover the same content area, but they are different tests with different registrations, timings, and score reporting[7].

Which states require Praxis 8004?

We have verified two states. West Virginia lists 8004 with a qualifying score of 147, and Arkansas lists 8004 with a qualifying score of 130[2][3]. Adoption status in the other 48 states is not verified, and we do not publish unverified adoption claims. Check https://praxis.ets.org/state-requirements/<yourstate>-tests.html before registering.

What is the passing score for Praxis 8004?

There is no national passing score. Each state sets its own qualifying score. Confirmed 8004 values are 147 in West Virginia and 130 in Arkansas[2][3]. Your state’s ETS requirements page is the authoritative source.

Does Praxis 8004 include multiple-select questions?

Yes. 8004 is the one test in the 8000 series for which ETS publishes multiple-select samples, and the official samples use the wording Which TWO of the following. Multiple-select items are graded as a set: both choices must be correct for the item to count[4].

Can I use a calculator on Praxis 8004?

No. ETS does not provide a calculator for 8004. The on-screen scientific calculator applies to the mathematics and science tests in the 8000 series, not to social studies[1].

When are scores released for Praxis 8004?

Score reporting for the Elementary Education Fundamentals tests began 2026-04-17[5]. Check the official test page for the release schedule that applies to your test date, because reporting dates are specific to each administration window[1].

Is this free practice test official?

No. Learndiag is an independent study tool and is not affiliated with ETS. Practice questions are original, written to the official content category definitions published by ETS, and are not official ETS test items[1].
