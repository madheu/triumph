Praxis 5001 vs 7001: What's Changing and Which One Will You Take

2026-08-16 · Reading time: about 3 min

If you're preparing to become an elementary teacher, you've probably noticed something confusing at the ETS website: there are now two versions of the Elementary Education: Multiple Subjects exam floating around. The old standby is the Praxis 5001 (with subtests 5002–5005). The newer kid on the block is the Praxis 7001 (with subtests 7002–7005). Some states still require the 5001 series. Others have already switched to the 7001 series. And a few allow a mix-and-match approach that can leave even seasoned advisors scratching their heads.

This article breaks down exactly what we know about the praxis 5001 vs 7001 transition, what is still unclear, and—most importantly—how to make sure you register for the right exam before you spend a dime on prep materials.

Why ETS Created a New Elementary Education Praxis Version

ETS launched the Praxis 7001 to modernize how reading instruction is assessed at the elementary level. The biggest driver behind the change is the national push toward the Science of Reading. Many states now want proof that new teachers understand phonological awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension—not just general language arts content.

The Praxis 5001 uses the Reading and Language Arts (5002) subtest, which covers reading, writing, speaking, and listening in a broader format. In many states, candidates who took the 5001 also had to pass a separate Teaching Reading exam (5205) to satisfy literacy requirements. That meant extra registration fees, extra study time, and for some candidates, a daunting constructed-response (essay) section.

The Praxis 7001 attempts to streamline this. Its first subtest—Teaching Reading (7002)—embeds the Science of Reading content directly into the Multiple Subjects battery. In states that have adopted the 7001 series, you typically do not need to take a separate Teaching Reading exam. That is the single most important reason the 7001 exists: it consolidates literacy assessment into the main elementary exam and removes the separate essay requirement.

Praxis 5001 vs 7001: The Known Differences

Let's look at what has actually changed versus what stayed the same.

Structure: Four Subtests, Individually Scored

Both exams are built on the same four-subject framework, and you must pass each subtest independently:

Content AreaPraxis 5001Praxis 7001
Reading / Literacy5002 – Reading & Language Arts7002 – Teaching Reading
Mathematics50037003
Social Studies50047004
Science50057005

The math, social studies, and science subtests (7003/7004/7005 vs. 5003/5004/5005) cover the same core concepts. For example, both math subtests test numbers and operations, algebraic thinking, geometry, measurement, data, statistics, and probability. Both social studies subtests focus on U.S. history and government, geography, economics, and world history. Both science subtests cover earth/space science, physical science, and life science.

The Reading Subtest: The Real Divide

This is where the two exams diverge.

Praxis 5002 (Reading & Language Arts) covers:

  • Reading foundations
  • Writing, speaking, and listening
  • General language arts pedagogy

Praxis 7002 (Teaching Reading) covers:

  • Phonological and phonemic awareness and emergent literacy
  • Phonics and decoding
  • Vocabulary and fluency
  • Comprehension of literary and informational text
  • Written expression

In other words, the 7002 is narrower and deeper on how children learn to read, while the 5002 is broader across all language arts. If your state requires the 7001, you need to be comfortable with terminology like orthographic mapping, decoding strategies, and explicit phonics instruction.

Timing Differences

The total test time is the same—4 hours and 35 minutes—but the allocation shifts:

  • Praxis 5001: 90 minutes for Reading & Language Arts, 65 minutes for Math, 60 minutes each for Social Studies and Science.
  • Praxis 7001: 110 minutes for Teaching Reading, 55 minutes each for Math, Social Studies, and Science.

The extra 20 minutes on the literacy section reflects the denser, more technical content in the 7002.

Cost

As of this writing, the full Praxis 7001 battery costs $220 when taken together. The 5001 pricing is similar. However, the real savings with the 7001 often come from not having to pay for a separate Teaching Reading (5205) exam, which can run an additional $100+ in many states.

Passing Scores Vary—Sometimes Dramatically

Here is where things get tricky. States set their own cut scores, and the numbers do not always move in parallel between the two exams.

For example, in Louisiana, the passing score for the Reading subtest dropped from 157 on the 5002 to 138 on the 7002—nearly a 20-point difference—while math, social studies, and science stayed the same. In Kansas, the 7001 replaced the older 7811 exam, with a transition window allowing the old exam until August 2025.

Bottom line: you cannot assume that a passing score on one version translates to the other. Always verify the exact cut scores on your state's official page.

What We Still Don't Know (And Won't Guess)

It is important to be honest about the limits of current information.

1. The full state adoption list is a moving target. As of mid-2026, confirmed adopters include Missouri, Louisiana, Kansas, Idaho, Iowa, and Washington (with some allowing both versions). But other states are in various stages of review, and ETS does not publish a single, definitive "switch date" calendar. Some states may never adopt the 7001 at all.

2. The 7001 may itself be replaced soon. ETS has announced the Elementary Education Fundamentals tests (8000 series), launching in Spring 2026, which are designed to eventually replace both the 5001 and 7001 series. The target retirement date for the current exams is August 2028, though ETS says it will "work closely with states to align timing with their specific transition needs." This means the 7001 could be a transitional exam for some states.

3. Exact blueprint differences are still emerging. While we know the broad topic categories for the 7002, ETS has not released exhaustive side-by-side item-level comparisons for every subtest. Prep companies are still calibrating their materials.

The honest takeaway: Any article that claims to know every detail of your state's specific timeline with absolute certainty is probably overselling. The only way to be 100% sure is to check directly with your state.

How to Check Which Elementary Education Praxis Version Your State Requires

This is the step that saves candidates from disaster. Here is the exact order of operations:

  • Start at ETS, but don't stop there. Go to ets.org/praxis/states and select your state. This will show you which tests ETS lists as accepted.
  • Cross-check with your state education department. Search "[Your State] Department of Education teacher certification Praxis requirements" and look for the official .gov page. State requirements can lag behind or differ from what ETS displays.
  • Confirm with your teacher prep program. Universities and alternative certification programs often have internal deadlines that are stricter than the state's. Some programs require the 7001 even if the state still accepts the 5001.
  • Check for "mix and match" rules. Louisiana, for example, allows candidates to combine subtests from the 5000 and 7000 series—as long as you have one passed subtest in each of the four content areas. Do not assume you must stick to one series for all four subtests.
  • Verify if a separate Teaching Reading exam is required. If your state uses the 5001, ask whether you also need the 5205 Teaching Reading exam. If your state uses the 7001, ask whether the 7002 satisfies the literacy requirement on its own.

Pro tip: Screenshot the requirements page on the day you register. State requirements can change mid-semester, and you want proof of what was required when you signed up.

The Study Material Trap: Don't Buy the Wrong Book

Here is the most expensive mistake candidates make: they see "Elementary Education Praxis" on a book cover, assume it covers their exam, and only realize halfway through studying that the book is for the wrong version.

If your state requires the 5001: You need materials aligned to subtests 5002–5005. If your state also requires the separate Teaching Reading (5205) exam, you need prep for that too.

If your state requires the 7001: You need materials aligned to subtests 7002–7005. The 7002 is not the same as 5002. A 5001 prep book will not adequately cover phonemic awareness, decoding, or fluency in the depth the 7002 demands.

Can you use 5001 materials for the 7001? Only partially. The math, social studies, and science subtests overlap heavily, so a solid 5001 book can still work for 7003–7005. But for the literacy portion, you must supplement with 7002-specific content—preferably materials that explicitly reference the Science of Reading and the five domains tested on the 7002.

Red flags when shopping for prep:

  • The cover says "Multiple Subjects" but does not list a test code (5001 or 7001).
  • The publication date is before 2024 and claims to cover the 7001.
  • The table of contents does not mention phonological awareness, phonics, or the Science of Reading (for 7002 prep).
  • The practice questions look like general reading comprehension instead of instructional scenario questions.

When in doubt, email the publisher and ask: "Is this book aligned to Praxis 5001 or 7001?" If they cannot give you a straight answer, keep your money.

Practical Study Tips for Both Exams

Regardless of which elementary education Praxis version you take, the preparation strategy is similar—though the 7002 requires extra attention to literacy science.

1. Attack Your Weakest Subject First

Both exams require you to pass all four subtests. If you are strong in math but weak in social studies, do not spend three weeks on math just because it feels good. The subtest you are most likely to fail deserves the most upfront time.

2. Use the Official ETS Study Companion

ETS publishes free Study Companions for every subtest. These documents list the exact content categories and sample question formats. Download the companion for your specific subtest codes (e.g., 7002, not just "Elementary Ed") and use it as your syllabus.

3. For the 7002: Master the "Big Five"

If you are taking the 7001, the Teaching Reading subtest is where candidates trip up. Focus on:

  • Phonological and phonemic awareness (hearing and manipulating sounds)
  • Phonics and decoding (letter-sound relationships, syllable types)
  • Vocabulary and fluency (automaticity, prosody, word recognition)
  • Comprehension strategies (monitoring, summarizing, questioning)
  • Written expression (spelling, grammar, composition development)

Know the difference between explicit and implicit instruction. Know when to use decodable texts versus leveled readers. The 7002 loves scenario questions that ask you to pick the best instructional strategy for a struggling reader.

4. For Math: Know When to Estimate

Both the 5003 and 7003 allow a calculator on some questions, but many questions are faster with estimation. Practice identifying when precise calculation is a trap and when "about 50" is close enough to pick the right answer.

5. Take Full-Length Timed Practice Tests

The 4-hour-35-minute total duration is long. Your brain will fatigue during the third and fourth subtests. Simulate test day at least twice before the real thing. If possible, take a diagnostic that mimics the exact timing of your version.

The Bottom Line: Verify Before You Study

The praxis 5001 vs 7001 decision is not about which exam is "easier." It is about which exam your state—and your preparation program—actually accepts. Registering for the wrong version means wasted money, wasted study hours, and delayed certification.

Before you buy a single book or watch a single prep video:

  • Check your state education department's official requirements.
  • Confirm with your EPP (educator preparation program).
  • Match every prep resource to the exact test codes you need.
  • Know your state's passing scores for each subtest.

ETS is in a multi-year transition, and the landscape will keep shifting through 2028. The candidates who survive this transition unscathed are the ones who verify first and study second.
