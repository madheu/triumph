Praxis Test Codes Explained: What 5001 and the 8000 Series Actually Are

2026-10-07 · Last verified 2026-10-07 · Reading time: about 6 min

If you have been staring at a list of four-digit numbers and no idea which one to register for, this page is the decoder. The Praxis elementary education codes look random because they encode a subject, a grade band, and a generation of the test all at once. Once you know how the pieces fit, the numbers stop being noise.

Here is the short version: 5001 is not a single exam. It is the code for a registration that bundles four separately timed subtests, and the newer 8000 series replaces those subtests one by one rather than all at once. Everything else is below, including where the number 969 comes from.

One thing this page deliberately does not do: list passing scores. Every state sets its own qualifying score, and those numbers change. For those, go to your state licensing agency or read the verified passing scores by state.

Where "969" Comes From

One more number you will see attached to Praxis that is not an ETS code at all: 969. It is the number of original practice questions in the Learndiag question bank — 969 written questions spread across the four subtests of the 5001, free to practise without a paywall.

So if you searched something like “test 969” and landed here, that is us: 969 is our question count, not a test code you need to look up. If you were looking for the actual exam, it is 5001, and the table above is what you need.

5001 Is a Package, Not an Exam

This is the single most misunderstood point. When you register for Praxis 5001, you are registering for one exam session that contains four separately timed subtests. You do not get a separate score for “5001” — ETS reports a scaled score for each of 5002, 5003, 5004, and 5005, and you pass the bundle by passing the ones your state requires.

You can also register for the subtests individually at $64 each instead of the $180 combined registration. Before you do that, though, confirm two things with your state or your program: whether the subtests may be split across separate test appointments, and whether passing them separately counts the same as passing the combined 5001. Some states require the combined test, and it would be a costly mistake to assume otherwise.

For what each subtest actually contains, see Praxis 5001 subtests explained, or work through the full Praxis 5001 study guide.

5000 Series vs the 8000 Series

The two generations line up subject by subject: 5002→8002 for reading and language arts, 5003→8003 for mathematics, 5004→8004 for social studies, 5005→8005 for science. The newer 8000 series is being adopted state by state rather than all at once, so which one you take depends entirely on what your state or program lists — not on which one is newer.

One trap worth naming: 8002 and 8006 are not the same test. 8002 is reading and language arts content. 8006 is teaching reading — the newer counterpart to 5205. States that list 8002 generally do not also list 8006. See 8002 vs 8006 and 5001 vs the 8000 series for the full picture, or the 8000-series state requirements list for who requires what.

How the Codes Are Built

Once you know the pattern, the numbers stop being arbitrary. ETS built the elementary education codes around four data points, and each one tells you something.

PositionWhat it encodesExample
Leading digitContent area family5xxx = elementary education; 7xxx = the earlier elementary series; 8xxx = the newest 8000 series
Middle digitGrade band0 in 5001–5005 and 8002–8006 = elementary (K–6 surface)
Last two digitsSubject within the family01 = the whole bundle; 02 = reading; 03 = math; 04 = social studies; 05 = science; 06 = teaching reading

This is why 5002 and 8002 are the same subject — only the leading digit changed, because only the test generation changed. It is also why 5006 does not exist but 8006 does: teaching reading entered the 8000 series under a slot the 5000 series used for a different arrangement, which is exactly why people conflate 8002 and 8006.

One practical consequence: when a form or a program lists a code, read the subject first, not the number. A number you recognise as “math” from one generation may sit under a different last-two-digit slot in the next. The Praxis elementary education overview lists which subjects each generation covers.

Single Subtest or the Full 5001 Bundle?

This is the decision most candidates get wrong, and it is a money question: the combined registration is $180, the individual route can be $64 per subtest. Here is how to think it through.

SituationWhat to doWhy
Your state lists 5001 as the requirementRegister for 5001The combined code is what they named; subtests alone may not satisfy it
You are retaking one subject you failedRegister for that single subtestYou only re-sit the part you missed; check whether your state credits partial passes
You want flexibility on timingSubtests separately, if your state allows itSpreading the sessions lowers the per-day load, especially for math
You are close to test day and need all four anywayRegister for 5001One appointment instead of four; less scheduling risk
Your program lists specific codes like 5003Register exactly that codeDo not substitute a bundle for a named subtest — the transcript must match

Two cautions before you split it up. First, unselected time does not carry between subtests — 5002 gives you 90 minutes for 80 questions and 5004 gives you 60 minutes for 60, so finishing early on one does not buy time on the next. Second, some states and programs treat separate subtest passes as not equivalent to the combined credential. That rule varies and it is not negotiable after the fact, so confirm it in writing before you register.

Common Mix-Ups

  • “5001 is a whole test I take once.” No — it is a registration that bundles four timed subtests, and you get a separate scaled score for each.
  • “I need 8002 or 8006, they are the reading test.” They are two different subjects. 8002 is reading and language arts; 8006 is teaching of reading.
  • “Is969 a Praxis test code?” No — 969 is the size of the Learndiag question bank, not an ETS code. The test is 5001.
  • “5001 is being replaced, so I must switch.” The 5000 series is still sold and still accepted in many states. Your state or program names the code that decides it, not the calendar.
  • “If I pass all four subtests separately I have the 5001.” That depends on your state and program. Confirm before registering.
  • “ETS sets the passing score.” ETS sets the scale; each state sets the qualifying cut score.

Cost and Timing, at a Glance

  • Praxis 5001 combined: $180 — covers 5002, 5003, 5004, and 5005 in one session
  • Individual subtest: $64 each — register separately, subject to your state’s rules
  • Total content: 245 questions, about 4 hours 35 minutes — but each subtest is timed on its own, and unused time does not carry over

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Praxis 5001 test?

Praxis 5001 is the Elementary Education: Multiple Subjects exam. Code 5001 is a single registration covering four separately timed subtests: 5002 Reading and Language Arts (80 questions, 90 minutes), 5003 Mathematics (50 questions, 65 minutes), 5004 Social Studies (60 questions, 60 minutes), and 5005 Science (55 questions, 60 minutes). That is 245 questions in roughly 4 hours 35 minutes. The combined registration is $180; each subtest separately is $64.

What is the Praxis 969?

969 is not an ETS test code — it is the number of original practice questions in the Learndiag question bank. Those 969 questions are spread across the four subtests of the Praxis 5001 and are free to practise without a paywall. The actual elementary education exam is code 5001, with subtests 5002, 5003, 5004, and 5005; the reading test in the older framework is 5205, and the newer 8000-series codes run from 8002 to 8006.

What is the difference between 5002 and 8002?

They cover the same subject: reading and language arts. 5002 belongs to the older 5000 series, 8002 to the newer 8000 series. Which one you take depends on what your state or program lists. Do not confuse 8002 with 8006 — 8006 is Teaching Reading, the newer version of 5205, not of 8002.

Can I take the Praxis 5001 subtests separately?

You can register for each subtest individually at $64 each instead of the $180 combined 5001. Whether you may spread them across separate appointments, and whether passing them separately satisfies your requirement, depends on your state and your program. Confirm before registering — some states require the combined test.

How many questions are on the Praxis 5001?

245 in total: 80 for 5002, 50 for 5003, 60 for 5004, and 55 for 5005. Each subtest is timed separately, so you cannot carry unused time into the next one.

Do I need 8006 or 5205?

It depends on your state. 5205 is Teaching Reading: Elementary in the older framework; 8006 is Teaching Reading in the 8000 series. They cover the same subject area across two generations, so most states require one or the other, not both. A few states instead require 8002, which is reading and language arts — a different subject from teaching reading.
