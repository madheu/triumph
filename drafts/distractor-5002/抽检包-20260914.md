### 5002-001 — Reading and Language Arts / Reading  （正常样本）

**题干**：Which text structure organizes information by listing reasons and supporting details about a single topic?

- ✅ A. Description
- 　 B. Cause and effect
- 　 C. Problem and solution
- 　 D. Sequence

**正确答案**：Description

**正确答案解释**：A descriptive structure presents a topic and then lists characteristics, features, or supporting details. Cause and effect shows relationships between events, while sequence organizes events in time order.

**干扰项解释**：
- 「Cause and effect」→ Reads 'reasons' as causal links; cause and effect explains why events happen, not a topic's supporting details.
- 「Problem and solution」→ Equates supporting details with solutions; this structure centers on a problem and its fixes, not a topic's attributes.
- 「Sequence」→ Assumes listing means ordering; sequence arranges events or steps in time, which the stem never mentions.

### 5002-008 — Reading and Language Arts / Reading  （audit-A 曾疑其键带前导空格 —— 实测干净）

**题干**：Which punctuation mark correctly joins two independent clauses without a conjunction?

- 　 A. Comma
- 　 B. Apostrophe
- 　 C. Colon
- ✅ D. Semicolon

**正确答案**：Semicolon

**正确答案解释**：A semicolon can connect two closely related independent clauses without a coordinating conjunction. A comma alone creates a comma splice. A colon introduces explanation or list. An apostrophe shows possession or contraction.

**干扰项解释**：
- 「Comma」→ Creates a comma splice; a comma alone cannot join two independent clauses without a coordinating conjunction.
- 「Colon」→ Misapplies a colon; colons introduce lists or explanations rather than linking two related statements as equals.
- 「Apostrophe」→ Confuses possession with clause joining; an apostrophe marks ownership or contraction, never separates clauses.

### 5002-068 — Reading and Language Arts / Writing, Speaking, and Listening  （A 轮 MINOR：原解释只说"不够优雅"，未点出真正错误）

**题干**：Which revision clearly indicates that Mike made the mistake in 'Tom told Mike that he made a mistake'?

- ✅ A. Tom told Mike, 'You made a mistake.'
- 　 B. Tom told Mike that Tom made a mistake.
- 　 C. Tom told Mike that he had made a mistake.
- 　 D. Tom said that a mistake had been made.

**正确答案**：Tom told Mike, 'You made a mistake.'

**正确答案解释**：Directly quoting Tom as saying 'You made a mistake' identifies Mike as the person who erred. Naming Tom instead changes the meaning, and the pronoun 'he' remains ambiguous.

**干扰项解释**：
- 「Tom told Mike that Tom made a mistake.」→ Shifts the blame to the wrong person; naming 'Tom' as the one who erred contradicts the intended meaning that Mike made the mistake.
- 「Tom told Mike that he had made a mistake.」→ Keeps the ambiguous 'he'; shifting to past perfect does not resolve who made the mistake.
- 「Tom said that a mistake had been made.」→ Dodges the pronoun with passive voice; the revision erases ambiguity by also erasing who made the mistake.

### 5002-172 — Reading and Language Arts / Reading  （B 轮 MINOR：原解释子命题说得过满）

**题干**：Students memorize plot events but cannot analyze themes. What skill gap exists?

- ✅ A. They lack abstraction skills—moving from concrete events to universal ideas
- 　 B. They don't understand vocabulary
- 　 C. They read too much
- 　 D. They need more practice reading

**正确答案**：They lack abstraction skills—moving from concrete events to universal ideas

**正确答案解释**：Theme identification requires abstract thinking—students must extrapolate from specific events to general messages.

**干扰项解释**：
- 「They don't understand vocabulary」→ Targets word knowledge when the gap is abstraction; the student already follows the events but cannot generalize from them to universal ideas.
- 「They read too much」→ Treats practice as the problem; more reading without abstraction work will not produce theme analysis.
- 「They need more practice reading」→ Repeats the same kind of practice; moving from events to universal ideas needs explicit instruction.

### 5002-174 — Reading and Language Arts / Reading  （B 轮 MINOR：两个干扰项本身近义，解释也近义）

**题干**：How does setting influence a story's mood?

- 　 A. Only dialogue matters
- 　 B. Mood is unrelated to setting
- ✅ C. Atmospheric details (weather, time of day, location) create emotional tone
- 　 D. Setting has no effect

**正确答案**：Atmospheric details (weather, time of day, location) create emotional tone

**正确答案解释**：A stormy night in a decaying mansion creates dread; a sunny meadow creates peace—setting shapes emotional atmosphere.

**干扰项解释**：
- 「Only dialogue matters」→ Excludes description altogether; dialogue conveys mood alongside, not instead of, atmospheric detail.
- 「Mood is unrelated to setting」→ Denies the connection the question asks about; weather, time, and place are classic mood-builders.
- 「Setting has no effect」→ Setting alone cannot build mood; the same place reads as ominous or serene only through the atmospheric details an author supplies.

### 5002-183 — Reading and Language Arts / Writing, Speaking, and Listening  （B 轮 MINOR：末句逻辑衔接不顺）

**题干**：Why is the writing process recursive rather than linear?

- 　 A. Because editing comes first
- ✅ B. Because writers often revisit earlier stages (revising leads to rethinking, which leads to reorganizing)
- 　 C. Because there is no process
- 　 D. Because it follows strict steps

**正确答案**：Because writers often revisit earlier stages (revising leads to rethinking, which leads to reorganizing)

**正确答案解释**：Writing involves ongoing cycles of drafting, evaluating, and revising—not a simple one-way sequence.

**干扰项解释**：
- 「Because editing comes first」→ Reverses the sequence; generating ideas precedes polishing, while recursion comes from writers looping back to earlier stages.
- 「Because there is no process」→ Confuses recursion with absence of structure; writers cycle through identifiable stages.
- 「Because it follows strict steps」→ Asserts the opposite of recursion; a strict one-way sequence is exactly what writers abandon when they loop back.
