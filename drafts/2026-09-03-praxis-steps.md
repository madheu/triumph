# Praxis Steps, Explained: Modular Testing for the Elementary Education Fundamentals Tests

*2026-09-03 · Last verified 2026-09-03 · Reading time: about 8 min*

> **Test requirements vary by state, preparation program, and effective date. Confirm your required test code with your state licensing agency or educator preparation program before registering.**

**Praxis Steps** is ETS's modular testing approach for the new Elementary Education Fundamentals tests (8002–8006). Instead of sitting a full 100-minute subject test, you can take — or retake — a test one content category at a time, in smaller sections ETS calls "Steps." Steps became available for the 8002–8005 tests in **September 2026**; 8006 follows in **2027**[3].

This page explains what Steps are, what they cost, when retaking a single Step makes sense, and — the part nobody else covers honestly — how to find out whether your state actually uses them.

## What Praxis Steps Actually Is

The Elementary Education Fundamentals tests can be taken two ways[1][3]:

1. **The full test, in one sitting** — the same single-appointment format as any Praxis subject test. This has been available since the new series launched on March 9, 2026[4].
2. **As Steps** — each test's official content categories become individually schedulable sections. For example, Elementary Education Fundamentals: Mathematics (8003) can be taken as one 68-question test, or as three separate Steps: Numbers and Operations, Algebraic Thinking, and Geometry, Measurement, and Data[2].

Steps are not a different exam. The content, the scoring scale, and the qualifying scores your state sets are the same. What changes is the packaging: you demonstrate the same content knowledge in smaller, separately scheduled pieces, and ETS reports performance at the Step level — which means diagnostic feedback that points at the specific category where you struggled[1][5].

Three official mechanics matter in practice:

- **Each Step is a separate appointment.** You can order up to four tests or Steps in one transaction, but you cannot take multiple tests or Steps in a single sitting[2].
- **Steps have their own test codes.** If your state has adopted Steps, your State Requirements page lists the individual Steps and their codes alongside the full test[2].
- **You can mix paths.** If you took a full test before Steps opened, you can still take individual Steps of that test afterwards[2].

## What It Costs — and When Steps Actually Save You Money

The official standard pricing, effective June 1, 2026[2][4]:

| Item | Price |
| --- | --- |
| Full test (any of 8002–8006) | $79.00 |
| Individual Step | $39.50 |
| 3 tests | $149.50 |
| 4 tests | $199.50 |

ETS notes pricing may vary and special discounts may exist at times; your State Requirements page shows current pricing[2].

Here is the arithmetic ETS's marketing glosses over. Using 8003 (three Steps) as the example, these are **our calculations from the official fees above, not ETS figures**:

| Scenario | Full-test path | Steps path |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You pass everything on the first try | $79 | 3 × $39.50 = **$118.50** |
| You fail one category, retake once | $79 + $79 = **$158** | $118.50 + $39.50 = **$158** |
| You fail two categories | $79 × 3 = **$237** | $118.50 + (2 × $39.50) = **$197.50** |

Read that honestly: **if you expect to pass everything first try, the full test is $39.50 cheaper.** Steps break even if you need one retake, and only win if you need two or more. The "save time and money" claim in ETS's materials is real, but it is a retake argument, not a first-attempt argument[5].

Two things money does not capture:

- **A failed Step is a shorter defeat.** Retaking one category costs less preparation time and less test-day anxiety than re-sitting a full 100-minute test for a category you already passed[1][5].
- **Passing Steps bank progress** toward demonstrating the same content the full test covers — but how your state or program counts Step-based results toward licensure is set by them, not by ETS. That is the next section.

## State Acceptance: The Question We Will Not Answer For You

Whether Praxis Steps count toward your license depends entirely on your state licensing agency or educator preparation program. ETS is explicit that Steps are adoption-dependent: on the official test pages, Steps availability is framed as "**if the state where you are seeking licensure adopts this test**," and individual Steps only appear on a candidate's State Requirements page after their state has adopted them[2][3].

**What we have verified: nothing, yet — and we label it that way on purpose.**

We have not confirmed Praxis Steps adoption for any individual state as of 2026-09-03. We do not publish adoption claims we have not confirmed against a state agency or ETS state requirements page, because a wrong answer here costs you a test fee, a 28-day waiting period, and possibly a certification deadline.

**How to check your own situation in about five minutes:**

1. Open your state's ETS requirements page: `https://praxis.ets.org/state-requirements/<state>-tests.html`. (State names are written as one word, no hyphen — `westvirginia`, not `west-virginia`.)
2. Look for the Fundamentals test you need. If individual Steps are listed alongside the full test **with their own test codes**, your state has adopted Steps[2]. If only the full test appears, your state either has not adopted Steps or has not been added yet — in both cases, plan around the full test.
3. If your program (not the state) sets your testing requirement, ask your program directly whether Step-based results are accepted. ETS's infrastructure supports it; the acceptance decision is your program's[1][2].

We maintain a state-by-state tracker for the 8000 series and will publish verified Steps adoption there as it is confirmed — see [Praxis 8000 series state requirements](/praxis-8000-series-state-requirements) (in progress).

## Retaking a Single Step: The Rules Nobody Else Explains

This is where the 28-day policy gets subtle, and where guessing wrong costs a month. All from ETS's official Steps FAQ[2]:

- The standard Praxis retake rule applies: **once every 28 days**.
- **Full test first?** If you take the full test, you cannot take an individual Step *of that same test* until the 28-day period passes. So "I failed one category of the full test, let me just retake that piece next week" is not a thing — the 28-day clock applies.
- **Step first?** If you started with Steps, you can take the *remaining* Steps of that test in less than 28 days — but you may not take the full test until 28 days have passed since your last appointment.
- Practical reading: the 28-day rule protects against burning attempts, but it also means your path choice (full vs. Steps) partially locks in. Switching directions is possible; switching *quickly* is not.

Other mechanics worth knowing before you register[2]:

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Cancellation | Cancel 3+ full days before your appointment: 50% refund |
| Rescheduling | $40 fee, no later than 3 days before the appointment |
| Scheduling Protection | Optional, $25, when scheduling any full test or Step |
| Accommodations | Approved accommodations for the full test carry over to individual Steps |
| Free score recipients | Up to 4, selectable per Step; recipients carry across Steps of the same test |

## Why 8006 Is Not Part of the September Rollout

Praxis Steps cover 8002–8005 from September 2026. Teaching Reading (8006) follows in **2027**[3]. Until then, 8006 is taken only as a full test.

If your requirement is 8006, nothing on this page changes your timeline yet: one sitting, 80 questions, $79[3]. We will update this page when ETS publishes the 8006 Steps date. See [Praxis 8006 Teaching Reading](/praxis-8006-teaching-reading) for what that test covers.

## A Note on ETS's Own Timeline Wording

We track ETS's published dates closely, and they have moved:

- The partner brochure (© 2025) said the tests would launch "Spring 2026" — and they did, on March 9, 2026[4][5].
- Steps were first announced as "Summer 2026"[4]; the current official test pages say **September 2026 for 8002–8005 and 2027 for 8006**[3], and ETS's Steps hub currently describes the capabilities as available[1].

Bottom line: for the tests and Steps alike, the official test pages are the current authority, and this page follows them. If you find a date here that contradicts an official test page, trust the test page and [tell us](/contact).

## FAQ

**What is Praxis Steps?**
Praxis Steps is ETS's modular testing approach for the Elementary Education Fundamentals tests (8002–8006). It lets candidates take or retake one content category at a time as a separately scheduled "Step," instead of the full test in one sitting[1][3].

**How much does a Praxis Step cost?**
An individual Step costs $39.50; a full Fundamentals test costs $79.00 (standard pricing since June 1, 2026). ETS notes pricing may vary — your State Requirements page shows current pricing[2][4].

**When did Praxis Steps become available?**
September 2026 for tests 8002–8005, per the official test pages. Steps for 8006 (Teaching Reading) follow in 2027[3].

**Can I retake just the Step I failed?**
Yes — that is the core feature. But the 28-day retake policy still applies, and its details matter: after taking a full test, you cannot take a Step of that same test for 28 days; after taking Steps, you cannot take the full test for 28 days (though remaining Steps of the same test are allowed sooner)[2].

**Does my state accept Praxis Steps?**
That depends on your state or educator preparation program, and Steps only appear on your State Requirements page if your state has adopted them[2][3]. We have not verified adoption for any state as of 2026-09-03 and do not publish unverified adoption claims. Check your state's requirements page before registering.

**Why can't I take Steps for Praxis 8006?**
ETS has scheduled Steps for 8006 in 2027[3]. Until then, 8006 is available only as a full test.

**Can I take a Praxis Step at home?**
Yes. Full tests and individual Steps can be scheduled at home or at test centers, following the same rules and availability as other Praxis tests[2].

**Is a Praxis Step a different test from the full 8002–8005?**
No. Steps cover the same official content categories as the full test, on the same scoring scale. What differs is the packaging — smaller, separately scheduled sections with Step-level score reporting[1][2].
